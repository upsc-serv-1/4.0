TOPIC 1. Salient Features of Indian Society, Diversity of India
1. Cultural Mosaic – Coexistence of distinct cultural groups maintaining identity while sharing space.
Example: At Cannes 2024, the Bharat Pavilion displayed regional crafts, reflecting India’s cultural
mosaic.
2. Pluralistic Ethos – Social belief in respecting multiple identities and traditions simultaneously.
Example: India’s 2025 school curriculum included lessons on minority religions, promoting
pluralistic ethos.
3. Syncretism – Blending of diverse religious and cultural traditions into new forms.
Example: The 2024 Urs festival in Ajmer attracts both Muslims and Hindus, showing syncretism.
4. Caste Fluidity – Increasing social mobility challenging traditional caste boundaries.
Example: In 2025 urban Bengaluru, inter-caste marriages are becoming more common, showing
caste fluidity.
5. Linguistic Nationalism – Using language identity as a political or cultural rallying point.
Example: Telangana’s 2024 push for Telugu education reforms reflected rising linguistic
nationalism.
6. Urban-Rural Divide – Economic, social, and cultural gaps between cities and countryside.
Example: The 2024 farm protests highlighted tensions rooted in urban-rural divide.
7. Inter-community Dialogue – Conversations between different social groups to foster peace and
understanding.
Example: Hyderabad’s 2025 peace forums worked to reduce religious tensions through inter-
community dialogue.
8. Cultural Commodification – Turning cultural heritage into marketable goods or tourism.
Example: Rajasthan’s 2024 festival circuit promoted folk dances for global tourists, a case of
commodification.
9. Identity Assertion – Groups publicly expressing their unique social or cultural identity.
Example: The rise of tribal language media in 2025 strengthened identity assertion in Northeast
India.
10. Social Stratification – Layering of society into hierarchical groups by class, caste, or status.
Example: Delhi’s 2024 housing crisis showed clear social stratification along economic lines.
11. Religious Pluralism – Acceptance and coexistence of multiple faiths within society.
Example: Kumbh Mela 2025 saw participation from all major Indian religions, embodying pluralism.
