| --- |

The **Vedic Age** in ancient Indian history is generally divided into the **Rig Vedic Age between 1500-1000 BCE,** and the **Later Vedic Age period between 1000-500 BCE**. The **transition** from the Rig Vedic to the later Vedic Age marked **gradual but distinct** socio-economic transformations:
1. a) Tribal** and **a relatively egalitarian** society with kinship based on tribal identity transformed into a **stratified** society with socio-economic **inequalities** and social identity increasingly tied to varna;**
   b) Occupational groupings, initially flexible, solidified into the **four-fold varna system** based increasingly on birth, with growing **social restrictions** especially on **Shudras.**
   c) religion evolved from a **naturalistic system** with simple prayers and sacrifices to a complex **ritualistic framework** that elevated the social profile of **brahmanas.**

2. a) Rig Vedic society, though patriarchal, allowed **relatively greater autonomy** to women including participation in **rituals** and access to **education** (Lopamudra, Ghosha). In the Later Vedic period **patriarchy** **entrenched** with the rule of **primogeniture and patrilineal inheritance**;
   b) Marriage norms became more regulated, with increased **emphasis on patrilineal purity** and the **institution of gotra**. **Widow remarriage** came to be increasingly disapproved. While **child marriage** began to emerge.
   c) **Women's status declined**  significantly as they **lost rights** to attend sabha and vidhatha, were denied Vedic education, and confined to **domestic roles**.

3. a) **Economic activities** shifted from **pastoralism** and **semi-nomadism** to settled agriculture as the principal livelihood;
   b) **cattle** wealth and cattle-raid warfare (**Gavishthi**) gave way to **land** ownership and **territorial** conflicts.c) **Crafts** such as chariot making, weaving, and pottery advanced with early signs of **urbanization**;
   d) the system of **voluntary offering** called 'Bali' transformed into organized taxation with a **fixed collection system**.

Certain elements of Vedic society, such as the **patriarchal family structure, the caste system, and Vedic** **rituals** and practices, continue to exert a **deep influence** on contemporary society. ***(290 words)***

## Q3 [Year: 2026]
**Question:** Both Buddhism and Jainism have played a significant role in conceiving and shaping the monuments  and their art in the Indian subcontinent. Elucidate.

#### Answer from ForumIAS

| **Approach:** • Introduce the answer by giving a brief background about the advent of Buddhism and Jainism. <br>• In the body, elucidate their role in conceiving and shaping the monuments and their art in the Indian subcontinent. <br>• Conclude suitably. |

**Buddhism and Jainism** are ancient Indian religious philosophies that flourished during the **5th century BC** in the northern parts of the country. Based on the teachings of **Gautam Buddha** and the **24 Jain Tirthankaras** (from Rishabhdev to Vardhaman Mahavir) respectively, they a**rose as a form of protest** against the prevailing **religious orthodoxy** and **Brahmanical supremacy**.

Apart from their obvious social function, Buddhism and Jainism have played a significant role in conceiving and shaping the **monuments and their art** in the Indian subcontinent. This is discussed below: **A.** **Buddhism**
1. **Stupa:**
   a) Dome shaped** structures that house the **relics** of Buddha/ other revered saints;**
   b) many components are deeply connected to **Buddhist philosophy** (e.g. Chhattri- tri-ratnas of Buddhism, toranas- important life events of Buddha);
   c) **carvings** on outer walls of Stupa depict **Jataka tales** (events from Buddha’s previous births). E.g. Sanchi Stupa

2. **Cave architecture:**
   a) Construction of congregational **worshipping halls** called **Chaityas** **(**Karla caves);
   b) **residential quarters** for Buddhist monks called **viharas (**Kanheri caves);
   c) **mural paintings** in caves depicting scenes from the **Jataka tales** (Ajanta caves).

3. **Temples:**
   a) Mahabodhi temple** in Gaya is a key Buddhist temple and is related to the attainment of **Enlightenment** by the Buddha;**
   b) built mostly using brick and hence had a major influence on the development of **brick architecture.**

**Fig. Schematic Representation of a Stupa**

**B. Jainism**
1. **Stupa (Chaitya):**
   a) Consist of a **cylindrical three-tier structure** resembling the **Samavasarana** (divine preaching hall of the Tirthankar);
   b) Jain **Ayagapatas** (stone offering) depict the **design of a stupa** (cylindrical shape, column in all corners, Dharmachakra- e.g. Vasu Ayagapata);
   c) most famous Jain stupa excavated at **Kankali Tila Mound** in **Mathura.**
**2.** **Temples (Jinalaya):**
   a) Generally consists of **two types** of temples- **Shikhar Bandi** (congregational prayers) and **Ghar** (personal worship);
   b) resemble in style to the **contemporary** **Buddhist/Hindu** architecture.
   c) **Maru-Gurjara style** temples inspired primarily by Jain traditions (e.g. Dilwara temples, Mt.Abu);
   d) **Chaumukh desig**n (deity faces all four directions) reflects the **victory** of the Tirthankar in the **four cardinal directions** and the entire cosmos (e.g. Ranakpur temple).

3. **Caves (Layana):**
   a) Rock-cut caves at **Ellora** (Rashtrakutas), **Khandagiri and Udaygiri** (King Kharvela) and **Sittanavasal** (Pallavas, Pandyas);
   b) functioned as **monasteries** or temples.

Both traditions employed **architecture as a medium** to instill their **spiritual teachings.** These architectural forms thus stand as lasting monuments to the enduring **philosophical** and **ethical values** of Buddhism and Jainism (peace, non-violence etc), capturing their **essence** in **physical form.** ***(390 words).***

**Landslide** is the movement of mass of **rocks, debris, or earth**, down the slope under the influence of **gravity**. According to GSI, about 0.42 million sq.km or **12.6% of India’s** land area is prone to landslides.

Among the **mountainous regions** of India, Western Ghats are the **second most** **vulnerable** to the threat of landslides (after the Himalayas). Their landslide vulnerability stems from the **following causes**:
1. **Natural reasons:**
   a) Increasing **high-intensity rainfall** events due to **climate change** (e.g., Wayanad 2024 landslides);
   b) geologically **fragile terrain** with highly jointed basaltic rocks and lateritic soils (fracture easily when saturated);
   c) steep slopes and **escarpments** (e.g., Irshalwadi landslide in Raigad 2023).

2. **Anthropogenic reasons:**
   a) Large-scale **land use changes** in ecologically fragile regions (for mining, monoculture plantations);
   b) deforestation** that loosens the topsoil layer;**
   c) **slope modification** due

to development activities (dam construction); d) blockage of **stream network** and **over-extraction** of groundwater **destabilizes slopes**; e) **encroachment** on unstable slopes (Pune landslide 2014).

The **NDMA Landslide Risk Management Strategy (2019)** recommends the following measures to mitigate their impact:
1. a) Landslide Hazard Zonation** (Landslide Atlas of India) through state-of-the-art technology (UAVs, TLS);**
   b) landslide **forecasting** (NLFC Kolkata, Bhusanket Web Portal, Bhooskhalan Mobile app) and **early warning system** (LEWS);
   c) **HRVC assessment** covering social, economic, ecological dimension;
   d) creation of **SPV** for landslide management;
   e) establishment of the **CLRSM.**

2. a) Formulation of **mountain zone regulations** including land use policies;
   b) **development control** **policies** in natural hazard prone zones (Gadgill panel report, Kasturirangan committee report);
   c) updating and enforcement of **building regulations** promoting **structural safety** in landslide prone areas and their incorporation into **government programmes** (PMAY).

3. a) Awareness campaigns** among all stakeholders (school curricula, community education programmes);**
   b) strengthening the **response framework** through capacity building and training of **vulnerable communities** (elimination of communication gaps, **financial incentives** through MGNREGS for small scale structural mitigation measures, mock drills).

Vulnerability of the Western Ghats to landslides is **exacerbated** due to climate change and **population** **pressures**. It can be mitigated through **collaboration** among all stakeholders and post disaster recovery in line with the **principles of Sendai Framework for DRR. (*****359*** **words)**

**Question:** “The Gupta Age is the golden age of ancient India.” Critically examine.

| **Approach:** • Introduce the answer by giving a brief background about the Gupta rule in ancient India. <br>• In the body, examine whether the Gupta Age was a golden age of ancient India, providing both positive and negative aspects of the same. <br>• Conclude by highlighting the enduring influences of the Gupta empire. |
| --- |

**Gupta empire** was established in **eastern UP and Bihar** by **Srigupta** in the **late 3****rd** **century AD**. At its zenith, the Gupta Empire exercised authority over large parts of **northern and central India.**

This period has often been characterised as the **Golden Age of Ancient India** due to the following reasons:
1. a) Emergence of a **centralized authority** after the decline of the Mauryan empire restored **political** **stability** across northern India;
   b) **able rulers** who conquered **regional principalities** (Samudragupta), defeated **rulers of ‘foreign origin’** (Chandragupta II- Scythians) and repelled **foreign** **invasions** (Skandagupta- Hunas);
   c) significant **decentralization** (provincial autonomy) in administration.

2. a) High-levels of **economic prosperity** from growth in trade (with China and Roman Empire), reflected in the large number of **gold coins** issued;
   b) **newer lands** brought under cultivation (Brahmadeya) and introduction of **newer technologies** in agriculture.

3. a) Significant developments in l**egal administration** (civil and criminal law demarcated, codification of new law books- Brihaspati Smriti);
   b) remarkable progress in **art** (Ajanta paintings) and **architecture** (Dashavtar Temple) due to **royal patronage** (Samudragupta depicted playing Veena on his coins).

4. a) Growth of **secular literatur**e (Abhijananashakuntalam by Kalidasa, Mrichchchhakatika by Bhasa) with **Sanskrit** reaching its pinnacle (Amarkosha);
   b) increased compilation of **religious literature** (Ramayana, Mahabharat, Puranas) preserved **ancient knowledge**.

5. a) Unprecedented developments in **science and technology** (astronomy- heliocentric model by Aryabhatta, mathematics- invention of zero, medicine- Sushruta Samhita);
   b) significant advancements in **metallurgy**(Meharaulli Iron Pillar);
   c) promotion of **educational institutions** (Nalanda).

6. a) Social status** of Shudras and women improved (could listen to recitation of epics and Puranas);**
   b) enhanced **economic opportunities** for women from lower varnas (agriculture, domestic services);
   c) great degree of **religious tolerance** shown by Gupta rulers.

However, the following developments **contradict** the “Golden Age” narrative:
1. a) Large-scale **land grants to Brahmanas** re-established their **social supremacy**;
   b) advent of landed elite **strengthened feudal tendencies** at the cost of centralized authority;
   c) a**dministrative posts** started becoming **hereditary** which weakened royal control over bureaucracy.

2. a) Growth of **rigidity in caste system (**increase in the number of **untouchables)** and associated discrimination (Chandals had to announce their arrival by striking a piece of wood);
   b) emergence of newer **social evils** (first Sati recorded in 510 AD) emanating from the **strengthening of patriarchal** **attitudes** (women treated as husband’s property).

3. a) Concentration of wealth** in the hands of a few (ruling elite, brahmins, merchants) causing widespread **economic inequality (slavery);****
   b) decline in **long-distance trade** from political instability during the **later Gupta** period (Huna invasion, succession conflicts).

Despite its debated status as the ‘Golden Age of Ancient India’, the Gupta age played a **significant historical** **role.** Its profound **enduring influences** (zero, decimal system) continue to impact modern human civilization. (***441 words***)

## Q7 [Year: 2026]
**Question:** Indo-Persian culture flourished during the era of the Delhi Sultanate. Discuss with reference to  developments in the fields of art, architecture and literature.
*Metadata: [Year: 2026] [Institute: Forum IAS] [Programme: MGP] [is_pyq: false] [Group: ] [Exam: ] [Stage: Mains] [Paper: GS1] [Subject: HISTORY] [Section Group: Art and Culture] [Micro Topic: Indian Culture-Salient aspects of Art Forms, Literature and Architecture from ancient to modern times.] [Sub Topic: Kingdoms & Highlights] [Macro Tag: Analytical, Descriptive] [Micro Tag: Discuss, Indo-Persian culture, Delhi Sultanate, Art, Architecture]*
#### Answer from ForumIAS

| **Approach:** • Begin by providing a brief overview of the Delhi Sultanate period. <br>• In the body, discuss how this era witnessed the emergence of Indo-Persian culture, with reference to developments in literature, art, and architecture. <br>• Conclude by emphasizing how this cultural synthesis created a unique legacy that continued to evolve over time. |
| --- |

The period of the **Delhi Sultans,** spanning from the **early 13th century to the early 16th century**, encompassed **five major dynasties** viz. The **Mamluks, Khilji, Tughlaqs, Sayyids and Lodhis**. While the period is significant as it strengthened the **foundations** **of Islamic rule** in India, it also saw **Indo-Persian culture** flourishing in the subcontinent.

This was particularly reflected in the fields of **art, architecture and literature** in the following ways:
**1.** **Art**
   a) i) Inherited the rich tradition of **music from Iran and Central Asia**;
       ii) brought **new musical** **instruments** (rabab, sarangi), modes and regulations (Perso-Arabic ragas like aiman, ghora, sanam by Amir Khusrau);
       iii) the period marked the emergence of **differentiation** in music modes from **north** **and south India.**

   b) i) **Translation** of Indian classical works i**nto Persian** (Ragadarpan under Feroz Shah Tughlaq);
       ii) development of **miniature painting** by merger of Indian and Persian elements (Lodi Khuladar style prevalent in Sultanate dominant regions between Delhi and Jaunpur);
       iii) popularization of **Persian** **calligraphy** styles (Naskh, Nastaliq) for writing manuscripts and decorating buildings.

**2.** **Architecture**
   a) i) Emergence of the I**ndo-Islamic architecture** through confluence of Indian styles with Persian styles;
       ii) conversion of many **existing places of worship into mosques** (Quwwat-u-Islam Mosque, Adhai din ka Jhopda);
       iii) geometrical vegetal ornamentation **(Arabesque method)** and verses from Quran **(calligraphy)** carved in an artistic manner **(tomb of Iltutmish);**
       iv) usage of **Hindu motifs** (bell, bel, lotus, swastika).

   b) i) Usage of **arch and dome** (Arcuate style, e.g. Alai Darwaja);
       ii) extensive use of **red sandstone and** **mortar** as a cementing agent;
       iii) strengthening of the structure through sloping walls **(batter** **technique**);
       iv) usage of both the principles of **arch and lintel, and beam** in later stages (Tughlaq, Lodis).

   c) i) Introduction of intricate **Jaali works**;
       ii) construction of major **cities** (Siri, Tughlakabad) with the **fortified citadels** (Ferozshah Kotla) and **reservoir complex**(Hauz Khas);
       iii) development of **regional** **schools of architecture** e.g., Bengal school (Adina Masjid), Jaunpur school (Atala Masjid).

**3.** **Literature**
   a) i) **Persian** was adopted as the l**anguage of administration**;
       ii) historical records, court chronicles, poetry, and prose were written in Persian, e.g. Tabaqat-i Nasiri by Minhaj Us Siraj;
       iii) development of a strong school of **history writing** (Barani, Isami);
       iv) emergence of **Amir Khusrau** as the most notable Persian writer of the period, created a **new literary style in Persian** (sabaq-i-Hindi). His major works include Ghurrat-ul-Kamal, Tughlaq Nama, Khazain-ul-Futuh.

   b) i) **Interactions between Sanskrit and Persian**, e.g. Suka Saptati (Tutinama) translated to Persian by Zia Nakshabi;
       ii) many **Sanskrit works translated to Persian** during the reigns of Feroz Shah Tughlaq (Dalai-i-Firoz Shahi);
       iii) many **Sufi saints** blended Persian literary traditions with local cultural elements (Nuzhat al-Majalis by Jamali Kamboh).

By facilitating Indo-Persian interactions, the Sultanate **institutionalized cultural hybridity** as a mode of statecraft. This approach not only **stabilized its rule** but also produced long-term **socio-cultural templates** later adopted by regional and imperial powers like the **Mughals.** *(****484 words)***

## Q9 [Year: 2026]
**Question:** Explain the process of formation of twisters. Why are the majority of twisters observed in areas around  the Gulf of Mexico?
*Metadata: [Year: 2026] [Institute: Forum IAS] [Programme: MGP] [is_pyq: false] [Group: ] [Exam: ] [Stage: Mains] [Paper: GS1] [Subject: GEOGRAPHY] [Section Group: Physical Geography & Geophysical Phenomena] [Micro Topic: Important Geophysical phenomena such as earthquakes, Tsunami, Volcanic activity, cyclone etc.,] [Sub Topic: Twisters] [Macro Tag: Descriptive, Analytical] [Micro Tag: Explain, Why, Twisters, Gulf of Mexico]*
#### Answer from ForumIAS

| **Approach:** • Introduce the answer with the definition of twister. <br>• In the body, first explain the process of formation of twisters. <br>• Then, analyze the reasons for the occurrence of the majority of twisters in regions around the Gulf of Mexico. <br>• Conclude with highlighting the measures taken to prevent disastrous impacts of twisters. |
| --- |

A **twister**, commonly known as a **tornado**, is a violently rotating column of air that extends from a thunderstorm to the ground. They are **characterized** by their **funnel shape**, with a wider upper portion connected to **cumulonimbus clouds** and a narrower lower portion that touches the ground.

Twisters are formed through **complex meteorological processes** as explained below:
1. The formation of twisters begins with the **convergence** of warm, moist air near the ground. This air, being buoyant, **rises rapidly** due to its lower density compared to the surrounding air. As the air ascends, it **diverges aloft**, creating an upward motion.

2. **Wind shear** (changes in wind speed and direction with altitude) causes the rising air to **start spinning** resulting in the formation of a rotating column of air. A **trigger** mechanism, often a disturbance like **intense ground heating or collision of** **contrasting air mass**, initiates this process by providing the necessary energy to lift the warm, moist air.

3. Atmospheric **instability** (when normal lapse rate is higher than the adiabatic lapse rate) further **promotes strong updrafts**, enhancing the upward motion of the air. At the surface, low-pressure systems, known as **surface cyclogenesis**, develop and contribute to upward motion. These conditions lead to the formation of powerful thunderstorms known as **supercells**.

4. Within these supercells, a f**unnel cloud begins to form**. This funnel cloud **extends downward** from the thunderstorm. When the funnel cloud **reaches the ground, it becomes a tornado**, completing the formation process.

The majority of twisters are observed in in areas around the **Gulf of Mexico** due to the **following reasons**:
1. **Geographical Location** i.e. the Great Plains provide a **flat terrain** where **cold, dry air from the** **Rockies or Canada** meets **warm, moist air from the Gulf of Mexico**. The strong collision of air masses creates turbulence along the boundary, leading to the formation of eddies. These eddies develop into powerful rotating wind whirls, allowing warm, moist, and unstable air to rise rapidly.

2. Twisters are most common in this area from **April to September**, corresponding to the **high sun** **season** in the northern hemisphere. The intensely heated **ground radiates heat** upward which provides **energy to the ascending** warm air.

While twisters are formed through complex meteorological processes, their prevalence around the Gulf of Mexico is influenced by unique geographical conditions. **Effective disaster preparedness** and **response** **measures** are essential to mitigate the impacts of these powerful natural phenomena. ***(400 words)***

***Additional Information*** Characteristics of a twister: **Funnel shape**, with a wider upper portion connected to cumulonimbus clouds and a narrower lower portion that touches the ground; **extremely low central pressure** (often around 100 mb lower than the surrounding environment); **high wind speeds** (ranging from 400 km/h to 800 km/h);**erratic movement** (typically move at 40-60 km/h but can become stationary or exceed 100 km/h); **narrow paths** (usually a few meters to over 2000 meters wide); **varying** **spatial and temporal** impact (average travel path 40-50 km, lasting 15-20 minutes or several hours); **dark** **appearance** (due to dust and debris); and often **preceded by dark clouds and low pressure**, associated with violent **thunderstorms.**

According to recent reports, the Indian Ocean has witnessed an **increase in SST(**~0.15°C/decade between 1951 and 2015). This has led experts to suggest that the tropical Indian Ocean is likely to be in **a near-permanent** **heatwave state** (expected to heat up further 1.7°C–3.8°C from 2020–2100). This is due to the following **reasons**:
1. a) Enhanced **radiative forcing** due to increase in **GHG emissions** from anthropogenic sources (fossil fuel burning);
   b) increasing trend in **global average OHC** due to the **heat sequestration** function of global oceans (absorbed ~93% of additional heat).

2. **Transfer of heat** from the Pacific Ocean via the **Walker circulation** and **ITF**. This is due to the recent negative phase of IPO which **strengthened the Pacific Easterlies**. It has, in turn, led to decrease in heat transport from the western to eastern Indian Ocean, creating a **zonal heat gradient**.

3. a) Rise in temperature of water received from the **Southern Ocean** and via the **deep meridional** **overturning circulation** (global warming);
   b) **seasonal changes in ocean dynamics** (southwest monsoon, seasonal upwelling in Atlantic, changes in thermocline depth) causing basin wide warming and impacting regional heat distribution;
   c) contribution of **interannual climate anomalies** (IOD, ENSO).

This unprecedented warming is expected to have the following **multi-dimensional impacts**:
1. a) Exacerbation of **developmental challenges** (food security, disaster vulnerability) in **rim countries** (mostly low/middle income);
   b) disproportionate impact on **coastal communities** (salt-water ingress, flooding) and **ecosystems** (wetlands, mangroves) from **rise in sea-water levels.**

2. a) Increased **regional and temporal variability** of monsoon (decline in northern India, reduced during onset, increased during withdrawal);
   b) widespread **extreme rainfall events** over Western Ghats and central India due to rapid warming of Arabian Sea;
   c) disruptions to key **ocean-atmosphere coupled** **phenomena** (IOD, MJO).

3. a) Altered **global weather patterns** (droughts in South America);
   b) frequent and intensified **natural** **disasters** (tropical cyclones, landslides, heatwaves);
   c) steep escalation in **MHWs** and its cascading effects (rapid intensification of cyclones, destruction of kelp forests).

4. a) Expected changes in the **bio-geochemical characteristics** (pH, oxygen), affecting the **marine** **ecosystem** (coral bleaching);
   b) suppressed **nutrient mixing** (enhanced thermal stratification) causing potential **decline in phytoplankton population**. This can have devastating consequences for **marine food web**, impacting the **blue economy**.

5. a) Temporary slowdown in global warming due to **accelerated sinking of heat** in Northern Atlantic (warmer Indian Ocean strengthens the AMOC);
   b) impact on the ability of Pacific Ocean to absorb heat (strengthening of **Pacific easterlies,** increased frequency of **El-Nino Modoki**).

Addressing the warming of the Indian Ocean demands a **multifaceted approach**. This primarily involves **reducing global carbon emissions** and building **systemic resilience** (forecasting capabilities, adaptive agriculture). The **UN High Seas Treaty 2023** offers a starting template in this direction. (***446 words***)
