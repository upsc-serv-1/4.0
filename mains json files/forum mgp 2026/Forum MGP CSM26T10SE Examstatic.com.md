#### Answer from ForumIAS

| **Approach:** • Introduce the answer by giving a brief account of the Skill India Mission. <br>• In the body, first highlight the factors responsible for the gap between vocational education and employability. <br>• Then, point out the successful interventions by the mission. <br>• Conclude by, suggesting reforms for strengthening the mission. |
| --- |

**Skill India Mission** was launched in **2015** to create and implement **comprehensive skill development training** programmes and build a **skilled, future-ready** workforce. However, according to the Economic Survey 2023- 24, **only about 52 per cent** of the country's youth are **deemed employable.**

The gap between **vocational education and employability** in India can be attributed to the following **interconnected factors**:
1. a) **Fragmented governance** with over **20 ministries** operating more than **40 skill development** **schemes** often with limited coordination ;
   b) **implementational challenges** due to constrained **financial resources** and over-reliance on public funding;
   c) **outdated curriculum** that fails to incorporate emerging technologies and industry trends.

2. a) **Skill mismatch** between industrial requirements and available trained workforce;
   b) **weak industry-** **academia linkages** resulting in education focused more on theory than **practical application**;
   c) **inadequate certification** and standardization mechanisms that lack industry recognition undermining **credibility** of training.

3. a) **Regional imbalances** in skill development infrastructure and opportunities;
   b) **limited access** to quality training in rural and remote areas (only 3.4% people in rural areas received formal vocational education, compared to 7.2% in urban areas - PLFS (2023));
   c) **social stigma** attached to vocational education compared to **traditional academic pathways.**

4. a) **Limited focus** on **soft skills** and **workplace readiness** in traditional vocational training programs;
   b) **lack of career counseling** and guidance for skill selection based on aptitude and market demand;
   c) **insufficient focus** on **entrepreneurship** and **self-employment** as viable career options;
   d) **gender** **gap in skilling** (low female enrolment in vocational courses as compared to male) leading to **low** **female labour force participation.**

However, the mission has made **significant strides** in the last decade, invigorating the skill development ecosystem in India in the **following ways**:
1. a) Has facilitated skill development (~1.5cr candidates trained till June 2024) by providing free **short-** **duration skill training** and incentivizing youth for **skill certification** (PMKVY);
   b) provided **vocational** **training** to non-literates, neo-literates as well as school drop-outs in rural regions by identifying skills that have a relevant market in that region (JSS).

2. a) Facilitated **end-to-end support to artisans and craftspeople** (e.g. certification, credit) who work with their hands and tools (PM Vishwakarma Yojana);
   b) aided in **income diversification of rural poor** families, while catering to the **career aspirations of rural youth** (DDU-GKY).

To further capitalize on the country’s  **demographic dividend**, the mission must evolve by incorporating industry-aligned **curriculum (co-designing** with private sector participation), quality training **infrastructure**, standardized **certification** (aligned with NSQF), and focus on **future-ready skills such as AI, green jobs, etc.** (***366 words***)

## Q3 [Year: 2026]
**Question:** Poverty and malnutrition create a vicious cycle, adversely affecting human capital formation. What  steps can be taken to break the cycle?

#### Answer from ForumIAS

| **Approach:** • Introduce the answer by explaining interplay of poverty and malnutrition. <br>• In the body, suggest comprehensive steps to break this vicious cycle. <br>• Conclude by highlighting the SDGs associated with poverty and malnutrition. |
| --- |

Poverty and malnutrition are deeply **interconnected,** creating a vicious cycle that hampers human capital formation. Malnutrition impairs **cognitive development**, educational attainment, and labor **productivity**, thereby facilitating **intergenerational transmission of poverty**. Poverty restricts **access to nutritious food** and **healthcare**, reinforcing the **malnutrition trap**. Govt’s ‘**Poshan Tracker’** data shows that as of **May 2025**, **37%** of children (0-5 years) were **stunted** and **16 % were underweight.**

**Interventions at multiple levels** are required to break this cycle:
**1.** **Food security, nutrition, and healthcare:**
   a) Enhancing **dietary diversity,** through promotion of **nutrition-sensitive agriculture**, community/kitchen gardens, and local food systems;
   b) address **micronutrient deficiencies** and **hidden hunger (**food fortification);
   c) improve **maternal and child** **nutrition** (POSHAN-Abhiyaan);
   d) promote **exclusive breastfeeding** for the first six months;
   e) ensure universal access to quality **primary healthcare (AAMs)**;
   f) improve **WASH infrastructure** and practices to prevent diseases.

2. **Education, skills and livelihood**:
   a) **ensure universal access** to quality education (Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan) and address **gender disparities** in education (Beti-Bachao-Beti-Padhao);
   b) **industry-** **specific** skill training programs (Skill-India) for livelihood generation (PMKVY, Tata-STRIVE);
   c) promote **digital literacy** to address digital divide (PMGDISHA, Google's Internet Saathi).

3. **Economic empowerment**:
   a) Create **income-equity** for the marginalized and poor (MGNREGA);
   b) promote **entrepreneurship** among vulnerable (PMEGP, Startup-India);
   c) enhance **financial inclusion** (PM-Jan Dhan, NABARD's SHG-BLP);
   d) provide **social safety-nets** (PM-KISAN, NSAP).

4. **Social empowerment**:
   a) address **gender-inequalities** and **double burden** (Gender-Budgeting, Taaron ki Toli-Initiative);
   b) curb perpetuation of **patriarchy, systemic deprivation** and **violence**

against women (Sashakt Nari – Viksit Bharat programme; Strengthening legal frameworks like DVA, ); c) tackle **social exclusion** ( Project Excel, Naandi Foundation) and empower **marginalized communities** (PMAY-G, ActionAid-India); d) strengthen **community institutions** (NRLM, Kudumbashree).

5. **Environmental sustainability:** a)promote **sustainable-agriculture**, organic farming and agroecology (NMSA);
   b) address **climate change impact** to manage reduced yield and production (NAPCC);
   c) ensure access to **clean energy** (PM-Ujjwala, PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana);
   d) community training to improve **water management** (JJM).

6. **Governance:**
   a) enhance **policy coherence** (Government, Civil society, Private-Sector);
   b) improve **service delivery** (e-governance, DBT);
   c) strengthen **local governance** (capacity building of ULBs);
   d) promote **evidence-based policymaking**.

**Breaking the cycle** of poverty and malnutrition requires a **multi-faceted approach** involving nutrition, healthcare, education, economic empowerment, and governance reforms. This will also strengthen our progress toward achieving **SDGs 1, 2, and 3.** ***(355 words)***

## Q4 [Year: 2026]
**Question:** Amid the recent controversy over alleged electoral roll related-malpractices, the debate on  mandatory Aadhaar-Voter ID linkage has regained attention. What is your opinion in this regard? Justify  your answer.
*Metadata: [Year: 2026] [Institute: Forum IAS] [Programme: MGP] [is_pyq: false] [Group: ] [Exam: ] [Stage: Mains] [Paper: Mains - GS 1] [Subject: Polity] [Section Group: Elections & Political Dynamics] [Micro Topic: Salient features of the Representation of People's Act] [Sub Topic: Electoral reforms] [Macro Tag: Analytical, Applied] [Micro Tag: What is your opinion, Justify, Aadhaar-Voter ID linkage, India]*
#### Answer from ForumIAS

| **Approach:** • Introduce the answer by giving a brief background about the recent controversies over alleged electoral roll related-malpractices and the proposal for Aadhar-Voter ID linkage. <br>• In the body, provide your opinion over the proposal and justify the same with valid arguments. <br>• Conclude by suggesting a way forward. |
| --- |

After the recently concluded **Maharashtra Assembly elections** (November 2024), several political parties and civil society organizations alleged **discrepancies related to voter registrations**. They also underlined their **consequences for public trust** in the electoral process.

The linking of Aadhar and Voter ID was **first tried by the EC** in February 2015 when it launched the **NERPAP** to address the **issue of duplicate entries** in the electoral rolls. However, the exercise was **suspended**  **after the** **SC** flagged the **mandatory use** of Aadhar for **non-welfare purposes**. **Aadhaar-Voter ID linkage** can have the following benefits in the **electoral process**:

1. a) **Weeding out duplication** of voters so that one citizen has only one vote, upholding the democratic principle of **"one person, one vote”**;
   b) may ease **voter mobility**, especially for **domestic migrants**, enabling them to participate in the democratic process at their place of residence.

2. **Aadhar-based verification** of biometrics (iris scan, fingerprint) offers a more **reliable method of** **voter identification**, reducing **impersonation fraud** while streamlining the **verification process** at polling stations (using fingerprint scanner to verify biometrics).

3. **Greater coverage of Aadhar** (>99% of Indian population covered by it) gives it **practical** **implementation advantage** over other **official identity proofs** (ration cards, driving licenses) for validation of electoral roll entries.

4. a) Aadhar-Voter ID linkage will also help **fulfill the legislative intent** expressed in Election Laws Amendment Act 2021;
   b) can act as the springboard for **future innovations** (remote voting, e-voting) that can improve **participation rates**.

However, there exist the following concerns with regards to **mandatory Aadhaar-Voter ID linkage**:
1. a) Apprehensions about **violation of informational autonomy** (little choice in deciding the official document they wish to use) and **privacy** (absence of a robust data protection framework);
   b) Aadhaar verifies **residence rather than citizenship**, creating a **fundamental mismatch** with voter eligibility requirements that primarily depend on **citizenship status.**

2. Possibility of high levels of **exclusion errors** (e.g. disenfranchisement of 3 mn voters in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in 2015), especially among **vulnerable sections**. This would infringe upon their constitutional **right to vote**.

3. a) The Supreme Court's **KS Puttaswamy judgment** placed restrictions on mandatory Aadhaar usage, making **blanket implementation** legally questionable;
   b) **centralization** of electoral and demographic data creates risks of **political misuse**(voter profiling) through **targeted voter suppression or** **manipulation**

While there are **multiple benefits** of Voter ID-Aadhar linkage, the **disadvantages necessitate a pause** in the process. This pause should be utilized to generate **voter awareness** about the benefits of this exercise and **remove infirmities in the Aadhar database**. At the same time, suitable **safeguards** should be instituted **to** **prevent misuse**. (***405 words***)

| --- |

**Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)** is a **National Mission on Financial Inclusion** encompassing an integrated approach. It envisages **universal access** to banking facilities with at least one basic banking account for every household, **financial literacy**, access to **credit, insurance and pension**.

Launched in **2014, it** has achieved several **milestones**, as discussed below:

1. a) **Banking the unbanked** (>55 crore new accounts- April 2025), especially the **rural and semi-urban** **population** (>36 crore accounts out of the total);
   b) created a robust **digital public infrastructure** with the integration of JAM trinity for direct delivery of benefits(e.g., MGNREGA).;
   c) eliminating ghost **beneficiaries,** plugging subsidy leakage and **improving fiscal prudence**.

2. a) Leveraged **technology platforms** to bridge **geospatial divides** (UPI, mobile banking);
   b) **fueled** **demand** for banking services leading to expansion of **banking outreach infrastructure** (43% increase in branches, 30% increase in ATMs, 724% rise in PoS terminals);
   c) women empowerment through **gender inclusion** in formal finance (>50% account holders are women) .

3. a) Promoted a culture of saving enhancing **financial security**;
   d) **mobilization of public savings** (>₹2.6 trillion in deposits) strengthened bank balance sheets, thus facilitating increased **investment and** **growth;**
   e) **expansion of social security nets** with increased access to **micro-insurance** (free accident insurance coverage of ₹ 2 lakh) and **pension schemes** (APY).

4. a) Catalyst in **funding of the unfunded** (PM SVANidhi, MUDRA scheme);
   b) **unconditional access** to financial services (no-frills zero-balance accounts);
   c) **affordable credit** and emergency access to funds (₹10,000 overdraft facility);
   d) **last-mile connectivity** through Bank Mitras;
   e) **aiding crisis** **response** (Rs.1500 financial assistance during COVID-19).

However, the scheme lags on a few fronts due to the **following shortcomings**:
1. a) Persisting **account dormancy** (21.17%- Jan 2025 according to RBI) undermines intended financial behavior;
   b) high **operational and maintenance cost** for banks threatening their **profitability** and long-term **sustainability** (low deposits);
   c) **limited financial literacy** (only 27%)  hindering full utilization of available services .

2. a) Access to **digital financial services** limited by **digital divide** (only 34% rural Indian households have access to internet, TRAI);
   b) limited access to certain **financial services** (e.g. overdraft facility, accidental insurance), due to **operational issues** (delay in issue of RuPay debit credits );

3. a) Increased **susceptibility to cyber frauds** due to **low digital literacy** and **weak cyber hygiene** **practices** of accountholders;
   b) functioning as **mule accounts** (accounts on rent) used by cyber gangs for carrying out **online scams** and **money laundering;**
   c) persistent **last-mile connectivity issues** in remote regions despite technological interventions.

These shortcomings can be overcome by expanding **financial literacy programmes (**e.g. “RBI Kehta Hai”), **incentivizing account usage** and **improving cyber security** among other measures. Adopting these measures shall further the goal of financial inclusion and economic empowerment as **"access to financial services is a** **fundamental right**, as basic as access to clean water". *(****452 words)***

2. a) Supporting transgender **organizations** and **communities** in their efforts to advocate for rights, provide resources, and create safe spaces;
   b) **institutionalizing sensitization programs** for key stakeholders (law enforcement agencies, religious leaders, political actors) to help change **societal** **attitudes**;
   c) allowing the avenues for **cultural expression** (e.g.  cross dressing).

3. a) **Rehabilitating** abandoned individuals (Garima Greh);
   b) ensuring access to required **health** **services** including **mental healthcare**;
   c) bringing about **change in perception** through their **economic** and **educational empowerment** (employment opportunities, financial rights, separate reservation);
   d) granting them **right to self-identification.**

The **Act** is undoubtedly a significant step in the direction of recognition of transgender rights. ## Q7 [Year: 2026]
**Question:** Despite steady economic growth and self-sufficiency in food production, India continues to grapple  with the issues of hunger and food insecurity. Examine the factors responsible for this paradox and  suggest policy measures to address them.
*Metadata: [Year: 2026] [Institute: Forum IAS] [Programme: MGP] [is_pyq: false] [Group: ] [Exam: ] [Stage: Mains] [Paper: Mains - GS 1] [Subject: Social Justice] [Section Group: Poverty & Hunger] [Micro Topic: Issues and Challenges relating to Poverty & Hunger] [Sub Topic: Hunger] [Macro Tag: Analytical, Applied] [Micro Tag: Examine, Suggest, Hunger, Food insecurity, India]*
#### Answer from ForumIAS

| **Approach:** • Introduce the answer by highlighting through data the challenges of hunger and malnutrition in India despite steady economic growth and self-sufficiency in food production. <br>• In the body, first examine the factors responsible for this paradox. <br>• Next, suggest policy measures to address them. <br>• Conclude by emphasizing the significance of combating hunger and food insecurity. |
| --- |

**India**, which is on track to become a **$5tn economy,** recorded a **historic production of foodgrain** during the year 2023-24 (3322.98 LMT). Yet, the **Global Hunger Index** ranks India 105th out of a total 127 countries, with “**serious”** levels of hunger.

This paradox stems from multiple **factors** which are listed below:
1. a) **Persistent poverty** limiting purchasing power for a significant population segment;
   b) **income** **inequality** creating substantial disparities in food access;
   c) Issues in food **supply chain management** (lack of sufficient **cold storage** for perishables, inadequate processing facilities for **dairy and fishery** products, concerns with FPS) leading to **inflation**.

2. a) **Policy driven** production pattern (MSP) contributing to decline in **cropping and dietary diversity** (wheat, rice);
   b) **skewed focus** on cereal production despite growth in **horticulture** (India ranked 2nd in vegetable and fruit production) and **livestock sectors** (CAGR of 12.99% from 2014-15 to 2022-23);
   c) **last mile delivery gap** (ghost beneficiaries, exclusion error, outdated beneficiary estimates) in PDS.

3. a) **Poor quality of food** prepared under welfare schemes (Mid-Day Meals);
   b) **structural issues** in the food storage infra (cover and plinth storage in FCI godowns leading to **rotting of grains**) reduces **availability of food grains;**
   c) poor **sanitation and hygiene** that impedes **effective assimilation** of nutrients.

4. a) **Gender-biased food distribution** within households increases women's vulnerability to malnutrition (higher prevalence of anaemia);
   b) low **“food and nutrition literacy (FNL)”** (only 64% children are under exclusive breastfeeding up to 6 months);
   c) inappropriate **eating practices** (binge eating), unhealthy **cooking methods** (reusing oil multiple times) and widespread availability of **HFSS** foods.

The challenges of hunger and food insecurity can be addressed through the following **policy measures**:
1. a) **Plugging leakages** in PDS (Chhattisgarh model);
   b) **reforms in food supply chain** (FCI reforms, e- NAM, cold storage, PM-AASHA, Kisan Rail);

However, this shift has the potential for **adverse impacts,** as discussed:
1. a) Rising cost of healthcare, driven by **profit-making** will make access to good quality care a privilege**;**
   b) irrational healthcare interventions** driven by **commercial considerations** [e.g. proportion of **caesarean deliveries** in India in private hospitals (48%) is three times higher compared to public hospitals (14%)];**
   c) widening of **urban-rural healthcare gap** due to concentration of private hospitals in urban regions.

   d) **e****rosion** of public healthcare system over time due to **neglect, underfunding,** and **underutilization**.

1. a) Increasing **public spending on healthcare** to meet the target of the NHP 2017 (2.5% by 2025);
   b) strengthening **primary healthcare infrastructure** (e.g. PHCs and CHCs);
   c) **leveraging private-sector** efficiency through PPP mode (e.g. empaneled hospital in PM-JAY);
   d) reducing OOPE through **universalization of health insurance** (PM-JAY);
   e) focusing on **preventive care** with health promotion at the community level, yoga and traditional medicine (WASH, AYUSH).

2. a) Increasing affordability** of health care through **dynamic pricing models** (e.g. Thailand’s tiered pricing system, which considers patient-income levels and medical necessity);**
   b) **standardizing** **medical procedure rates** across government and private sectors (SC recommendation);
   c) **centralized purchasing** i.e. combining purchasing functions across many public healthcare organizations to **drive down the prices** (leverage economy of scale) of services, technology and pharmaceuticals.

3. a) Paying attention to **overlooked aspects** such as mental health, geriatric and **palliative-care** (e.g. Kerala's Neighborhood Network for Palliative Care);

   c) establishing **intensive accountability framework** to ensure service-delivery to intended beneficiaries (e.g. community-based monitoring under NRHM).

4. a) Decentralizing regulation** to increase public-sector bureaucracies’ responsiveness to local conditions, (e.g. France creating relatively autonomous Regional Health Agencies);**

   c) **recruiting and retaining** healthcare professionals (e.g. incentive for rural posting under NHM).

Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS) are those programmes which **do not pertain to subjects in the Union List** but which are **funded directly by Central Ministries/Departments.** They are i**mplemented by States** or their agencies irrespective of their pattern of financing.

More than **80** **Centrally** **Sponsored** **Schemes** are currently operated by different Central Ministries/Departments. The proliferation of CSS has led some states to criticise and raise concerns about them. They are as follows:
1. a) Centrally **predetermined schemes** limits state’s discretion, leading to **progressive erosion** of the state authority (‘**annihilative federalism’**);
   b) increased **financial burden** on states through **mandatory cost-sharing** requirements;
   c) gradual **reduction in untied grants** has led to **shrinking** **fiscal space** for States (increased tax devolution by XIV Finance Commission squared off by greater contribution of States towards CSS).

2. a) **Uniform implementation models (**one-size fits all approach) limit states’ ability to tailor the schemes to local needs and contexts;
   b) **overlaps with existing** state schemes, lead to **duplication** of efforts and tension in federal relations (‘**confrontational federalism’)**, for e.g. Swasthya Sathi scheme (WB) vs PMJAY.

3. a) Delay in disbursement** of Centre's share and **non-release of funds** citing **non-compliance** (MGNREGS funds for West Bengal have been held up by Centre citing non-compliance since March 2022);**
   b) issue of **credit-sharing** over **rebranded state schemes** e.g. PM KISAN (KALIA scheme - Odisha).

**Following measures** can be taken to address these concerns:
1. a) Institutionalize a **comprehensive consultation process** involving state governments during scheme design;
   b) evolution of a **transparent** criterion for **inter-state fund allocation** (based on population, development needs);
   c) **flexibility in cost norms** across states and their inflation indexation;
   d) **streamlining** the process fund flow and utilization(leverage **technology**).

2. a) Rationalization** of the number of schemes (evolution of “umbrella schemes”);**
   b) schemes below a certain **allocation threshold** must be **justified** by the department concerned (XVth Finance Commission);
   c) having a reasonably **wide admissible pool** of components in a scheme to allow flexibility to state ;
   d) third party **monitoring and evaluation** of schemes’ outcomes (NITI Aayog) .

The evolving landscape of cooperative federalism demands a **dynamic reimagining** of Centrally Sponsored Schemes. As India navigates **complex developmental challenges**, adaptive and responsive governance becomes paramount to achieving **inclusive national progress.** ***(364 words)***
