## Ethics, Morals, Laws, Rules, Conscience

### PYQs
- **[2020]** Distinguish between laws and rules. Discuss the role of ethics in formulating them. *(10m)*
- **[2016]** Law and ethics are considered to be the two tools for controlling human conduct so as to make it conducive to civilized social existence. (a) Discuss how they achieve this objective. (b) Giving examples, show how the two differ in their approaches. *(10m)*
- **[2015]** A mere compliance with law is not enough, the public servant also has to have a well-developed sensibility to ethical issues. Explain with two examples where (i) an act is ethically right but not legally and (ii) an act is legally right but not ethically. *(12m)*
- **[2023]** Is conscience a more reliable guide when compared to laws, rules and regulations in the context of ethical decision making? Discuss. *(10m)*
- **[2021]** In case of a crisis of conscience, does emotional intelligence help to overcome the same without compromising the ethical or moral stand? Discuss. *(10m)*
- **[2019]** What is meant by 'crisis of conscience'? How does it manifest itself in the public domain? *(10m)*

### Quotes
- "A strong society is built not by strict laws alone, but by individuals with good character." - **Swami Vivekananda**
- "If people are just then laws are not needed." - **Aristotle**
- "Ethics is knowing the difference between what you have the right to do and what is right to do." - **Potter Stewart**
- "The end of law is not to abolish or restrain, but to preserve and enlarge freedom." - **John Locke**
- "Good people do not need laws, but bad people will always find ways to circumvent them." - **Plato**
- "Law sets the minimum standard of human behaviour while ethics sets the maximum standard."
- "There is a higher court than the court of justice and that is the court of conscience." - **Gandhi**
- "Courts of conscience are higher than courts of law." - **Gandhi**
- "The law is reason, free from passion." - **Aristotle**
- "To see what is right and not do it is a lack of courage." - **Confucius**
- *Satirical Inversion:* "Conscience is the inner voice that warns us somebody may be looking." - **H.L. Mencken** *(used to argue that ethical behaviour must be intrinsic, not observation-dependent)*

### Ethical Terms
- **Legal Rigidity vs. Ethical Relativism**: static law vs. evolving moral standards
- **Zombie Laws**: outdated legal provisions needing reform
- **Conscience**: inner voice aligning thoughts, words, and actions - source of moral intuition independent of external enforcement
- **Examined vs. Unexamined Conscience**: Examined conscience = tested, reflective, cultivated moral sense. Unexamined conscience = unreflective, culturally absorbed prejudice. Examined conscience > Law > Unexamined conscience
- **Crisis of Conscience**: moment when professional duty and personal moral conviction directly conflict - demanding a conscious ethical choice
- **Cognitive Dissonance**: psychological discomfort when one's actions violate one's conscience - integrity resolves this by closing the gap between knowledge and action
- **Constitutional Morality (Ambedkar)**: conscience elevated to constitutional obligation - not instinctive popular sentiment but cultivated democratic ethics.

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Hierarchy**: Examined Conscience > Law > Unexamined Conscience. Law is superior to social prejudice; examined conscience is superior to law when law is unjust.

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Vibhishana's Defection (Ramayana)**: Vibhishana left Lanka and joined Rama against his own brother Ravana - choosing Dharma over political loyalty. His examined conscience ranked higher than the law of kinship and the law of the land.
- **Yudhishthira and the Dog**: Chose to give up his own place in heaven rather than betray the dog. At that moment, the dog transformed into Dharma Deva revealing this was a final test of Yudhishthira's virtue.
- **Prahlada's Refusal**: refused to renounce his conviction despite Hiranyakashipu's torture - followed his examined conscience over the law of royal authority.

### Examples
- **Widespread tax evasion despite strict income tax laws**: laws cannot legislate morality
- **Dowry Prohibition Act**: reactive, not preventive: law's limitation
- **Forest Rights Act**: without impartial enforcement, loses force
- **Euthanasia laws remain restrictive while families often see withdrawal of life support as morally compassionate**: legal rigidity vs. ethical relativism
- **Oscar Schindler**: followed his conscience over Nazi law - saved 1,200 Jews at enormous personal risk. Law was on the side of the perpetrators; conscience alone pointed to right action.
- **Sachin Tendulkar walking off the field**: accepted an umpire's wrong decision by walking without protest - conscience over competitive advantage, without any legal compulsion.
- **Nuremberg Defence rejected (1945)**: soldiers could not use "following orders" as moral defence - the Nuremberg Trials established that conscience is not absolved by law.
- **Nelson Mandela's 27-year imprisonment**: refused to abandon his conviction even when offered release conditional on renouncing his cause - examined conscience outlasting institutional punishment.

### Civil Servants
- **T.N. Seshan (CEC)**: followed constitutional values beyond the legal minimum. His conscience elevated institutional standards above the letter of the law.
- **Satyendra Dubey (IES)**: followed his conscience over institutional loyalty - blew the whistle on NHAI corruption knowing the personal cost. His conscience was examined, tested, and paid for with his life.
- **Ashok Khemka (IAS)**: cancelled the DLF-Vadra land mutation following his conscience and constitutional duty despite knowing it meant punitive transfers - 57 transfers did not alter his examined conscience.
- **Kiran Bedi (IPS)**: introduced Vipassana in Tihar Jail against the conventional logic of prison administration - her conscience that prisoners deserve dignity drove institutional innovation.

## Normative Ethics (Deontology vs Teleology)

[Subject: ETHICS, INTEGRITY & APTITUDE]
[Section Group: Ethics & Human Values]
[Microtopic: Ethics and Human Interface: Essence, Determinants, Consequences, and Dimensions of Ethics]
[Subtopic: Normative Ethics (Deontology vs Teleology)]

### PYQs
- **[2018]** With regard to morality of actions, one view is that means are of paramount importance and the other view is that the ends justify the means. Which view do you think is more appropriate? Justify. *(10m)*
- **[2014]** Human beings should always be treated as 'ends' in themselves and never merely as 'means'. Explain the meaning and significance of this statement in the modern techno-economic society. *(12m)*

### Quotes
- "The greatest good for the greatest number is the foundation of morals and legislation." - **Jeremy Bentham**
- "Two things awe me most: the starry sky above me and the moral law within me." - **Immanuel Kant**
- "Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end." - **Immanuel Kant**
- "Morality is not the doctrine of how we may make ourselves happy, but how we may make ourselves worthy of happiness." - **Immanuel Kant**

### Ethical Terms
- **Deontology (Kant)**: ethics based on inherent rightness of actions regardless of consequences
- **Teleology / Consequentialism**: ethics based on outcomes and results
- **Utilitarianism (Bentham/Mill)**: greatest happiness of greatest number
- **Nishkam Karma**: Gita: humans control only means, not ends
- **Categorical Imperative (Kant)**: universalisability test
- **Virtue Ethics (Aristotle, Plato, Buddha)**: focus on character: "What would a virtuous person do?"
- **Golden Mean (Aristotle)**: virtue lies between two extremes
- **Moral Absolutism**: some actions are always wrong regardless of consequences (Kantian)
- **Moral Relativism**: rightness depends on cultural and situational context
- **Act Utilitarianism**: judge each individual act by its consequences
- **Rule Utilitarianism**: follow rules that generally produce best consequences
- **Double Effect Doctrine**: an act causing harm may be permissible if harm is unintended and good outweighs it (used in medical ethics and just war theory)
- **Contractarianism (Hobbes, Rousseau, Rawls)**: ethics grounded in what rational persons would agree to
- **Slippery Slope**: Justifying small evils for good ends eventually erodes institutional integrity

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Ashwatthama Incident**: Krishna asked Yudhishthira to proclaim Ashwatthama is dead - technically true of the elephant - to break Drona's will and end the war. A deliberate half-truth for a teleological end: establishing Dharma. The Mahabharata's own Tirukkural moment: falsehood takes the place of truth when it produces unblemished common good.
- **Karna's Kavach-Kundal**: Indra, in disguise, asked Karna for his divine armour and earrings - knowing it would make Karna mortal. Karna gave them willingly, honouring the duty of a host despite knowing the deception. The Mahabharata's dirty hands problem: integrity of giving (deontological) vs. consequences of self-destruction (teleological) - admirable and tragic simultaneously.
- **Bhishma’s Pratigya (Mahabharata, Adi Parva)**: Bhishma took a vow of lifelong celibacy so his father could remarry. A deontological commitment made in perfect good faith that locked an entire civilisation into its consequences — every evil of the Mahabharata flows from this single oath. The Mahabharata’s warning that rigid deontology without contextual wisdom destroys more than it protects: the right procedure producing the catastrophic outcome.
- **Gandhi's Dandi March**: violation of colonial Salt Law (illegal) but morally right resistance
- **Gandhi's rejection of WWII support**: refused to support war even for independence: deontological commitment to non-violence over teleological gain
- **Gandhi at Chauri Chaura (1922)**: Gandhi halted the entire Non-Cooperation Movement after 22 policemen were killed by protesters. Deontological reasoning: means of violence contaminate the end of freedom regardless of outcome. Opposed every utilitarian argument his colleagues made - the most dramatic Indian demonstration of means-ethics over ends-ethics.

### Examples
- **Medical triage during pandemic**: prioritise patients based on survival probability: utilitarian application
- **Slavery and Apartheid**: legally mandated but grave violations of human dignity: legally right, ethically wrong
- **Forest Rights Act**: balancing tribal rights (deontological duty) with environmental conservation (consequentialist outcome)
- **Compulsory vaccination during COVID-19**: ends-based welfare maximisation
- **EIA norms under pressure to dilute for faster clearances**: tension between teleological efficiency and deontological duty
- **Nuremberg Trials (1945)**: soldiers could not use "following orders" as defence: deontological duty overrides hierarchical command

## Ethics: Consequences and Determinants

[Subject: ETHICS, INTEGRITY & APTITUDE]
[Section Group: Ethics & Human Values]
[Microtopic: Ethics and Human Interface: Essence, Determinants, Consequences, and Dimensions of Ethics]
[Subtopic: Consequences of Ethics]

### PYQs
- **[2022]** Adherence to ethics in human actions would ensure smooth functioning of an organisation. What does ethics seek to promote in human life? *(10m)*
- **[2020]** Discuss the role of ethics and values in enhancing the three major components of Comprehensive National Power (CNP). *(10m)*
- **[2017]** Increased national wealth did not result in equitable distribution of its benefits. It has created only some 'enclaves of modernity'. Discuss. *(10m)*

### Quotes
- "Without commonly shared and widely entrenched moral values and obligations, neither the law, nor democratic government, nor even the market economy will function properly."
- "The good of an individual is contained in the good of all."
- "Increased national wealth did not result in equitable distribution of its benefits. It has created only some enclaves of modernity and prosperity for a small minority at the cost of the majority."

### Ethical Terms
- **Consequentialist Ethics**: the moral worth of an act is determined entirely by its consequences
- **Second-Order Effects**: unintended consequences of well-intentioned policies: ethical governance must anticipate them
- **Social Contract Theory (Hobbes-Locke-Rousseau)**: governance derives legitimacy from ethical obligations to citizens; broken social contract → legitimacy crisis
- **Externalities**: costs or benefits falling on those not party to a decision: environmental ethics centres on managing negative externalities
- **Trust Deficit**: systemic consequence of widespread ethical failure: erodes institutions, democracy, and markets
- **Intergenerational Consequences**: decisions today binding future generations: environmental and fiscal ethics

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Comprehensive National Power(CNP)** = Military Power + Economic Power + Soft Power (Ethics strengthens all three)

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Parashurama's Oath and Its Aftermath**: Parashurama vowed to exterminate the Kshatriya class 21 times - driven by righteous anger at his father's murder. His consequentialist justification: eliminating a corrupt ruling class. The result: he created a power vacuum filled by worse rulers. The Puranas' refutation of ends-justify-means logic - righteous intent cannot guarantee righteous outcome.
- **Lanka Under Ravana (Ramayana)**: Ravana's Lanka was materially the most advanced civilisation of its era - golden palaces, Pushpak Vimana, advanced medicine. His achievements were real. Yet his ethical failures - abduction, disrespect of consent, killing of Bali - destroyed everything. The Ramayana's answer to growth-vs.-ethics: no CNP is sustainable when built on ethical violation.

### Examples
- **Trust Deficit (UPSC 2014)**: rampant corruption causing institutional distrust; consequences: tax evasion, electoral cynicism, capital flight
- **"Enclaves of modernity" (UPSC 2017)**: IT boom benefiting 1% while 40% farmers in agrarian distress: unethical distribution of growth consequences
- **Consequences of ethical governance**: Kerala's HDI ranking despite low per-capita income: ethics in governance producing better human outcomes than raw GDP growth
- **Consequences of unethical governance**: Uttarakhand floods (2013): illegal construction in eco-sensitive zones; 5,000+ deaths: environmental ethics violation having catastrophic human consequences
- **Social Harmony as governance consequence**: Gujarat 2002 riots: failure of administrative ethics producing decades of social fracture
- **Global Financial Crisis (2008)**: consequence of "commerce without morality": systemic unethical behaviour destroying $22 trillion in global wealth
- **Patriotism in civil life (UPSC 2014)**: paying taxes, water conservation, civic participation: everyday ethical consequences sustaining national fabric

### Ethical Terms
- **Internal Determinants**: conscience, knowledge, intent, values, stage of moral development, voluntariness, attitude, moral reasoning, past experience, moral courage
- **External Determinants**: constitution and law, religion, family and upbringing, culture and traditions, judiciary, media and digital ecosystem, education, professional codes of conduct, peer influence, societal expectations, leadership, situational factors
- **Character Chain**: Thoughts > Values > Beliefs > Words > Actions > Character
- **Divine Command Theory**: religion offering God-based code of conduct
- **Gilligan's Ethics of Care**: Carol Gilligan's feminist critique: relationships and care as moral determinants, not just abstract principles
- **Social Learning Theory (Bandura)**: ethics learned by observing and imitating role models
- **Moral Disengagement (Bandura)**: psychological mechanisms people use to justify unethical conduct: rationalisation, dehumanisation, displacement of responsibility

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Banfield's Amoral Familism**: Underdevelopment = Amoral Familism -> Civic Morality -> Institutional Trust
- **Kohlberg's Stages of Moral Development**: Pre-conventional (rules + punishment) → Conventional (social norms) → Post-conventional (universal principles)

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Putlibai's influence on Gandhi**: Gandhi's attitudes of truth and non-violence rooted in mother Putlibai's daily religious discipline: family as determinant
- **Ambedkar's experience of caste**: Dr. Ambedkar's experience of caste humiliation shaped lifelong attitude against caste: personal experience as determinant
- **Tagore's Shantiniketan**: cultivated creativity and respect for nature: educational institution as determinant
- **Ashoka's transformation**: Ashoka's transformation post-Kalinga shaped by Buddhist philosophy of compassion: religion/culture as determinant
- **Chauri Chaura incident**: Gandhi ended Non-cooperation movement: conscience as internal determinant

### Examples
- **Milgram Experiment (1961)**: 65% of participants gave potentially lethal electric shocks on authority's command: situational factors overriding personal ethics; relevance to bureaucratic compliance with unethical orders
- **Stanford Prison Experiment (1971)**: ordinary students became brutal guards within days: environment as powerful determinant

### Quotes
- "In any moment of decision, the best thing you can do is the right thing, the next best thing is the wrong thing, and the worst thing you can do is nothing." - **Theodore Roosevelt**
- "The hottest places in hell are reserved for those who, in times of great moral crisis, maintained their neutrality." - **Dante**
- "It is not only for what we do that we are held responsible, but also for what we do not do." - **Molière**

### Ethical Terms
- **Gandhian Talisman**: test of whether a decision serves the poorest
- **Constitutional Morality**: Ambedkar's concept: cultivated commitment to constitutional values above popular or social morality
- **Gap Diagram**: space between "Form" (Procedure) and "Substance" (Justice) filled by a perceptive civil servant
- **Phronesis / Practical Wisdom (Aristotle)**: situational ethical judgment beyond mechanical rule-application
- **Tragic Dilemma**: choice between two options where both involve irreversible moral loss
- **Moral Remainder / Moral Residue**: the lingering guilt or regret after making the best available choice in a dilemma
- **Dirty Hands Problem**: even morally justified acts may involve moral cost (Walzer)
- **Precautionary Principle**: when in doubt, take the more cautious ethical path: especially in environmental and technology governance
- **Moral Reasoning**: deliberate, systematic logical justification of ethical judgments: slow, rule-based, consequentialist or deontological
- **Courage of Conviction**: willingness to act on ethical judgment despite personal, professional, or political cost

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Dual Newspaper Test**: Test 1 (unethical) + Test 2 (needlessly harsh)
- **Legality-Ethics-Legitimacy Triangle**: Action must fall at intersection of all three

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Arjuna's Vishada (Bhagavad Gita Chapter 1)**: Arjuna faced the archetypal ethical dilemma - Kshatriya duty vs. love for family. His collapse before the first arrow was shot is the Gita's opening dilemma. Krishna's resolution: act from dharmic obligation, not personal attachment - the Gita's answer to every bureaucrat torn between institutional duty and personal loyalty.
- **Karna's Final Choice**: Karna knew the Pandavas were his brothers but chose to fight for Duryodhana - honouring loyalty to the friend who raised him when all others rejected him. The Mahabharata's purest dirty hands problem: justice vs. loyalty, both legitimate, no clean resolution. Moral residue remains regardless of which path is chosen.
- **Vidura's Silence at Draupadi's Cheerharan**: Vidura, the wisest man in the Kaurava court, knew the dice game and its consequences were unjust but could not stop it - bound by his status as a state official. His silence is the Mahabharata's case study in institutional complicity: when a civil servant knows what is right but is structurally prevented from acting - the 2nd ARC's accountability gap is mythological.

### Examples
- **Gandhi ending Non-cooperation movement after Chauri Chaura**: immediate moral revulsion before rational calculation (Moral Intuition)
- **Supreme Court reasoning through Section 377 decriminalisation**: systematic logical justification through constitutional principles (Moral Reasoning)
- **Officer pressured to manipulate data for political agenda**: Constitutional Morality as "North Star"
- **Indian Army officer at LoC**: shoot first or wait: rules of engagement vs. moral obligation to protect soldiers
- **Collector during communal riots**: use force to control mob vs. risk escalation: classic administrative dilemma between action and restraint
- **A doctor in rural India**: one ventilator, two critical patients: medical triage as ethical dilemma in resource-scarce governance
- **2G Spectrum case**: DoT officials pressured to clear spectrum: dilemma between ministerial direction and institutional integrity
- **Conflict of Interest**: Chanda Kochhar-ICICI-Videocon case
- **Transparency**: RTI Act + proactive disclosure under Section 4; GeM portal reducing procurement opacity
- **Abortion and LGBTQ+ rights**: deemed immoral by some religions yet ethical: right to self-determination
- **NCERT civics embedding constitutional values through curriculum**: ethics through education
- **Indian Constitution's Preamble as a moral document**: Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity: ethics codified in law
- **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)**: global consensus on applied ethics post-WWII
- **India's Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)**: ethics codified as citizen obligations

### Civil Servants
- **Satyendra Dubey**: whistleblower's dilemma: chose public interest at ultimate personal cost
- **Probity in Public Life**: U. Sagayam publicly declaring assets; E. Sreedharan refusing to inflate project costs: procedural integrity in discharge of duty
- **Devotion to Duty**: Satyendra Dubey (IES): gave his life; Armstrong Pame: gave his personal resources; Ashok Khemka: gave his career

- **Spirit of Service**: S.R. Sankaran enforcing bonded labour abolition; Narendra Kumar (IPS) dying to stop illegal mining.

## Attitude & Practical Intelligence

[Subject: ETHICS, INTEGRITY & APTITUDE]
[Section Group: Psychology & Foundational Values]
[Microtopic: Attitude: Content, Structure, Function, Moral and Political Attitudes, Social Influence]
[Subtopic: Attitude for Civil Servants]

### PYQs
- **[2024]** Mindless addiction to Form, ignoring the Substance of the matter, results in rendering of injustice. A perceptive civil servant carries out true intent. Examine. *(10m)*
- **[2022]** The Rules and Regulations provided to all the civil servants are the same, yet there is difference in the performance. Positive minded officers interpret Rules in favour of the case. Discuss. *(10m)*
- **[2015]** Differentiate between bureaucratic attitude and democratic attitude. *(12m)*

### Quotes
- "The art of being wise is the art of knowing what to overlook.": William James

### Ethical Terms
- **Practical Intelligence (Sternberg)**: ability to adapt to, shape, and select environments: the "street smart" dimension of civil service effectiveness
- **Phronesis (Aristotle)**: situational wisdom adapting universal principles to particular circumstances: highest civic virtue
- **Positive vs. Negative Attitude Toward Rules**: same rules, different interpretation: attitude as multiplier of governance capacity
- **Administrative Discretion**: legal space within rules where attitude and practical intelligence determine outcome
- **Bureaucrat Attitude**: literal rule application regardless of context → process as end
- **Democratic Attitude**: spirit of law interpretation prioritising citizen outcomes → process as means
- **Form vs. Substance (UPSC 2024 PYQ)**: mindless literalism vs. purposive interpretation: practical intelligence resolves this
- **Creative Maladjustment**: productive non-conformity within systems: positive attitude toward reform rather than passive compliance
- **Compassionate Governance**: governance that is both efficient and humane

## Nature of Values

[Subject: ETHICS, INTEGRITY & APTITUDE]
[Section Group: Ethics & Human Values]
[Microtopic: Human Values: Role of family society and educational institutions in inculcating values]
[Subtopic: Nature of Values]

### PYQs
- **[2015]** Social values are more important than economic values. Discuss with examples in the context of inclusive growth and sustainable development. *(12m)*
- **[2014]** All human beings aspire for happiness. Do you agree? What does happiness mean to you? *(12m)*

### Quotes
- "Try not to become a man of success, but rather try to become a man of value.": Albert Einstein
- "Values are like fingerprints. Nobodys are the same, but you leave them all over everything you do.": Elvis Presley
- "The measure of a life, after all, is not its duration, but its donation.": Peter Marshall

### Ethical Terms
- **Gandhi's Seven Social Sins**: diagnostic framework for identifying ethical crises in society
- **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam**: "the world is one family": universalist ethical value
- **Value Conflict**: competing values creating ethical dilemmas (liberty vs. security, development vs. environment)
- **Values vs. Norms**: values are beliefs about what is desirable; norms are shared rules of behaviour
- **Transcendent Values**: values transcending self-interest (compassion, justice): highest level of moral development
- **Capability Approach (Sen and Nussbaum)**: human values measured as freedoms and capabilities, not just income
- **Viktor Frankl’s Logotherapy**: life flourishes only when it has a purpose transcending the self: meaning as the highest human value

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Maslow's Hierarchy**: Physiological -> Safety -> Social Belonging -> Esteem -> Self-Actualisation
- **Sen's Capability Approach**: Development = Expansion of Substantive Freedoms (Capabilities)

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Daridra Narayana (Vivekananda/Gandhi)**: serving God through serving the poor
- **Bishnoi community protecting trees and wildlife in Rajasthan**: environmental responsibility as terminal value
- **Sikh langars during COVID-19**: compassion and social cohesion as shared value
- **Gandhi's non-violent struggle**: gave India global moral stature: soft power as expression of value system
- **Karna's Generosity**: Karna was born with divine kavach and kundal but gave them to Indra in disguise when asked - knowing it would cost him his invincibility. He gave away the one thing that could have saved his life. Karna traded his physical invincibility for a legendary legacy - The living embodiment of Lao Tzu's wisdom: the wise man does not lay up his own treasures; the more he gives to others, the more he has for his own.
- **King Rantideva's Sacrifice**: Rantideva had fasted 48 days. When food finally arrived, he gave it away - to a Brahmin, a starving man, a dog, and an outcaste who begged for water. He kept nothing. His prayer: let me take on the suffering of all beings. The Bhagavata's most radical statement on values - they are real only when they cost something.
- **Sudama's Humility at Dvarka**: Sudama, Krishna's destitute childhood friend, brought only a handful of beaten rice to the most powerful court in the land. His values - humility, gratitude, friendship - had not changed with poverty or Krishna's elevation. The Bhagavata's parable on intrinsic vs. circumstantial values: character is not adjusted to fortune.
- **Sabari’s Devotion**: Sabari, a low-born tribal woman, waited decades for Rama’s arrival, tasting each berry to give him only the sweetest. Rama accepted tasted fruit that caste convention would call polluted. Devotion, care, love are not the property of any social class. The lowest-born can hold the highest values.

### Examples
- **Ganga-Jamuna tehzeeb**: preserving Hindu-Muslim unity in North India: cultural tolerance as collective value
- **Verghese Kurien's positive attitude toward farmers' cooperatives created Amul White Revolution**
- **India's National Education Policy 2020**: explicitly identifies values: critical thinking, creativity, communication, collaboration (4Cs)

## Duty

[Subject: ETHICS, INTEGRITY & APTITUDE]
[Section Group: Psychology & Foundational Values]
[Microtopic: Aptitude and Foundational Values for Civil Service]

### PYQs
- **[2025]** One who is devoted to one's duty attains highest perfection in life. Analyse with reference to the sense of responsibility and personal fulfilment as a civil servant. *(10m)*
- **[2019]** Non-performance of duty by a public servant is a form of corruption. Do you agree? Justify. *(10m)*
- **[2014]** In the context of defence services, patriotism demands readiness to lay down one's life in protecting the nation. Is the concept of patriotism compatible with the idea of becoming a soldier of peace? *(12m)*

### Quotes
- "One who is devoted to one's duty attains highest perfection in life.": Bhagavad Gita
- "Every duty is holy, and devotion to duty is the highest form of worship.": Swami Vivekananda
- "Non-performance of duty by a public servant is a form of corruption.": UPSC PYQ (2019)
- "Karmanyevadhikaraste ma phaleshu kadachana" (You have the right to perform your duties but not to the fruits): Bhagavad Gita

### Ethical Terms
- **Svadharma**: duty aligned to one's own role and station; devotion to it as path to perfection (Bhagavad Gita)
- **Nishkam Karma**: performing duty without expectation of reward; selfless action (Bhagavad Gita)
- **Karma Yoga**: the yoga of selfless action; channelling intelligence and energy through duty
- **Devotion to Duty**: acting from a sense of obligation rather than for reward or recognition
- **Spirit of Service**: intrinsic motivation to serve beyond the letter of duty; transforms job into calling
- **Corruption of Duty (Edmund Burke)**: non-performance or distortion of official duty as a form of corruption

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Gandhian Talisman**: Does this action benefit the poorest and weakest person? The ultimate duty-test for public servants

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Bhagavad Gita Chapter 3 (Karma Yoga)**: Krishna instructs Arjuna to perform duty without attachment to results; foundational Indian philosophy of duty
- **Ashoka's Rock Edicts**: "Sabhi Manuche Proja Mam" (All subjects are my responsibility): royal duty as ethical imperative
- **Sanjaya’s Honest Reporting (Mahabharata, Bhishma Parva)**: Sanjaya was given divine vision to narrate the Kurukshetra war to the blind king Dhritarashtra. He reported every Kaurava death, every defeat, without softening truth for his master’s emotional comfort. The Mahabharata’s model of the civil servant as honest reporter: speak truth to power even when power does not wish to hear it. The original duty of dissent.
- **Mahavira's Ahimsa**: duty not to harm as the first principle of ethical conduct in public life
- **Vivekananda**: "Every duty is holy" - raised duty from obligation to spiritual calling
- **Svadharma (Bhagavad Gita 3.35)**: Better is one's own dharma, though imperfectly performed, than the dharma of another well performed. The Gita's principle of role-based duty: a civil servant's duty is defined by their office, not by personal preference or political instruction.
- **Nishkam Karma (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)**: You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions. The foundational principle for civil servants - act from duty, not from expected reward or career calculation.
- **Rama's Exile (Valmiki Ramayana)**: Rama accepted 14 years of exile without bitterness when Kaikeyi invoked Dasaratha's promise - duty to a father's word over personal entitlement to the throne. The Ramayana's proof that duty does not select comfortable obligations; it demands adherence at maximum personal cost.
- **Shravan Kumar's Filial Duty**: carried his blind parents on a palanquin on pilgrimage. Performed duty not from obligation but from love, without expectation of recognition. The Puranas' paradigm of Nishkam Karma in its most human form - duty as devotion, not compliance.

### Examples
- **Oscar Schindler**: performed moral duty at personal cost to protect 1,200 Jews: conscience over legal compliance
- **Doctors Without Borders (MSF)**: duty of care performed in conflict zones despite personal risk: professional duty as moral imperative

### Civil Servants
- **Satyendra Dubey (IES)**: performed duty to public interest over personal safety; blew the whistle on NHAI corruption; paid with his life
- **T.N. Seshan (CEC)**: performed constitutional duty of free and fair elections against political resistance; epitomised devotion to duty
- **E. Sreedharan (Metro Man)**: performed duty to deliver Delhi Metro on time and within budget despite political pressures; no deviation from professional obligation
- **Armstrong Pame (IAS)**: performed duty to connect 120 villages through crowdfunded road when official channels failed.

### Quotes
- "Integrity is doing the right thing even when no one is watching.": C.S. Lewis

- "Knowledge without integrity is dangerous and dreadful.": Samuel Johnson

### Ethical Terms
- **Cognitive Dissonance**: gap between belief and action: integrity eliminates it
- **Moral Authority to Lead**: legitimacy derived from consistent ethical behaviour, not rank
- **Neuroplasticity**: repeated ethical behaviour forming new neural pathways
- **Integrity Pact**: agreement between vendors and buyers not to engage in corrupt practices; overseen by Independent External Monitors (IEMs)
- **OECD Public Integrity Recommendations**: mandatory integrity vetting, risk-based audits, automated asset disclosures
- **Moral Residue**: the lingering ethical discomfort after choosing the lesser of two evils: sign of intact integrity
- **Integrity Testing (honey trap / integrity test)**: covert testing of officer integrity used by CBI and vigilance agencies
- **Asset Declaration**: mandatory public disclosure of assets and liabilities by civil servants: institutional integrity tool
- **Revolving Door Problem**: officials moving between regulatory roles and regulated industries: structural integrity threat
- **Intellectual Integrity**: presenting data objectively without manipulation: critical in policy making and judiciary
- **Institutional Stewardship**: directing intelligence and energy toward public good
- **Karma Yogi**: selfless channelling of intelligence and energy through duty (Bhagavad Gita)
- **Smart Criminal Effect**: high intelligence and energy amplifying scale of unethical acts
- **Corrosive Leadership**: ethical failures at the top copied throughout the organisation
- **Procedural Integrity**: adherence to due process as dimension of probity
- **ASP Model**: Aspirational, Strictness, Participation (framework making Code of Ethics owned by subordinates)
- **ISO 37001**: Anti-Bribery Management System standard
- **Social Audit**: democratic process empowering stakeholders to scrutinise government programmes
- **Jan Sunwai**: public hearing mechanism for grievance redressal (MKSS, Rajasthan)
- **Probity in Public Life**: combination of integrity, transparency, accountability, and objectivity in discharge of public duties (Nolan Committee)
- **Declarable Interest**: financial or personal interest an official must disclose before participating in a decision
- **Lokpal**: central ombudsman for complaints against public servants including PM (Lokpal Act 2013)
- **Lokayukta**: state-level ombudsman: Karnataka Lokayukta under Justice Santosh Hegde as gold standard

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Integrity Triangle (Buffett)**: Effective Leader = Intelligence + Energy + Integrity
- **Dangerous Leader** = Intelligence + Energy - Integrity
- **2nd ARC Accountability Formula**: Accountability = Answerability + Enforceability

### Indian Civilisational Wisdom
- **Ashoka the Great**: redirected high intelligence and energy through Dhammaghosha toward ethical governance
- **Yudhishthira's Yaksha Prashna**: A yaksha killed all four Pandava brothers and posed philosophical questions to Yudhishthira. His integrity in answering truthfully brought his brothers back to life. Integrity as the one value that outlasts all adversity.
- **Harishchandra's Ordeal**: the solar dynasty king who sold his wife, his son, and himself into servitude rather than break his word. Lost everything material; preserved everything moral. The Puranas' proof that integrity is not a calculation but a constitution - it does not bend under any pressure.
- **Prahlada's Refusal**: Prahlada refused to renounce his conviction despite his father Hiranyakashipu's threats, torture, and attempted murder. His integrity survived fire, poison, elephants, and the ocean.

- **Bernie Madoff**: Ponzi scheme: intelligence without integrity

## Corruption

[Subject: ETHICS, INTEGRITY & APTITUDE]

### PYQs
- **[2022]** Whistleblower who reports corruption runs the risk of grave danger and victimisation. What policy measures would you suggest to strengthen the protection mechanism? *(10m)*
- **[2014]** It is often said that poverty leads to corruption. However, affluent and powerful people also indulge in corruption. What are the basic causes of corruption? *(12m)*

### Quotes
- "The greatest corruption is the corruption of duty.": Edmund Burke
- *Kautilya's Fish and Water Analogy:* "Just as one cannot tell when a fish is drinking water, detecting when an official misappropriates is nearly impossible."
- "Corruption is authority plus monopoly minus transparency.": Robert Klitgaard *(formula for corruption)*
- "When the fish rots, it rots from the head.": Chanakya

### Ethical Terms
- **Collusive Corruption**: all parties benefit except the public
- **Coercive Corruption**: poor pay bribes out of survival necessity
- **Corruption of Omission**: non-performance as form of corruption
- **Sanskritization of Corruption**: normalisation and social acceptance of corrupt behaviour
- **Yogakshema (Kautilya)**: state's duty to ensure public welfare: undermined by corruption
- **Grand Corruption**: high-level state capture by elites vs. **Petty Corruption**: small bribes at delivery points
- **State Capture**: when private interests systematically shape state policy and law for private benefit
- **Regulatory Capture**: regulated industry gaining undue influence over its regulator
- **Moral Hazard**: taking greater risks because consequences will be borne by others: structural corruption driver

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **Klitgaard's Formula**: C = A + M - T (Corruption = Authority + Monopoly - Transparency)
- **Fraud Triangle**: Corruption Risk = Pressure + Opportunity + Rationalization
- **Dimensions of Corruption (2nd ARC)**: Corruption = f(Moral failure x Institutional failure x Social normalisation)

### Examples
- **Nirav Modi PNB Fraud**: sophisticated knowledge without integrity enabling systemic exploitation
- **Enron**: "Yes Man culture": honest dissent punished, collusive corruption rewarded
- **Aadhaar exclusions causing starvation deaths**: institutional apathy as corruption of duty
- **Aam Aadmi Party's mohalla clinics model**: transparency portals and community oversight reducing frontline corruption in health delivery
- **MNREGA wage theft in Rajasthan**: MKSS jan sunwais recovering wages: social audit as anti-corruption tool
- **Hong Kong ICAC model**: reduced perceived corruption from among the world's worst (1970s) to among the best: independent commission as gold standard
- **Singapore's Prevention of Corruption Act + high salaries for civil servants**: structural approach to corruption deterrence

### Civil Servants
- **Satyendra Dubey**: exposed National Highway project corruption; case for strong whistleblower protection
- **Lokayukta raids in Karnataka**: Justice Santosh Hegde's Lokayukta report (2011) on illegal mining: Rs 16,000 crore loss; exemplary institutional anti-corruption action

### Quotes

### Ethical Terms
- **Conflict of Interest**: situation where personal interests compromise professional duty
- **Bhishma’s Conflict of Interest (Mahabharata)**: Bhishma served as regent and supreme commander for the Kaurava throne while personally loving the Pandavas. He managed his conflict of interest perfectly by procedure — never acting for personal gain — yet his institutional loyalty over conscience enabled every injustice he witnessed. - **Recusal**: voluntarily stepping away from decisions with actual or perceived conflict
- **Fiduciary Duty**: public official as trustee of public interest (Paul Douglas)
- **E-Procurement Norms**: digital procurement reducing discretion and corrupt influence
- **Nepotism**: favouring relatives, undermining meritocracy
- **Primacy of Public Interest**: public good takes precedence over private gain
- **Actual Conflict of Interest**: private interest directly influences official duty
- **Perceived Conflict of Interest**: appearance of conflict even if no actual influence: equally damaging to public trust
- **Cooling-Off Period**: mandatory gap between regulatory role and private employment in regulated sector
- **Divestiture**: selling financial interests to eliminate conflict before taking office
- **Chilling Effect**: self-censorship by officials fearing conflict-of-interest allegations: overcaution as governance cost

### Formulas / Frameworks
- **6R/4R Approach**: Recognize, Reveal, Remove, Relinquish

### Examples
- **IL&FS crisis**: independent directors compromised due to personal affiliations: breach of impartiality
- **Satyam scandal**: promoter-controlled board acting as rubber stamp: conflict between fiduciary duty and personal interest
- **Chanda Kochhar case (ICICI-Videocon)**: sanctioned loans in exchange for family investments: textbook conflict of interest in banking
- **India's revolving door problem**: senior TRAI officials joining telecom companies: structural conflict of interest
- **SEBI chairperson controversy (2023)**: allegations of investment in regulated entities: regulatory conflict of interest
- **Justice J. Chelameswar recusing from Aadhaar bench citing personal views**: judicial conflict of interest managed through recusal
- **Cabinet Secretary refusing to attend meetings on his own department's tenders**: administrative recusal as best practice
