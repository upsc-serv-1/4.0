## Issues Emerging from the PYQs
*   ***Welfare vs rights-based approach*** - The discriminatory-by-nature PYQ (2023) and the legal-aid PYQ (2023) test whether the State acts out of benevolence or honours justiciable entitlements under the Constitution.
*   ***Awareness and participation deficit*** - The 2019 PYQ links weak scheme performance to the absence of beneficiary awareness and active involvement across the policy cycle, not just to design or funding.
*   ***Disability as the unfinished agenda*** - The two RPwD PYQs (2022, 2017) show that a strong statute - 21 disabilities, 4% job reservation - fails without sensitisation, accessibility and grievance redress.
*   ***Gender empowerment beyond schemes*** - Women's social capital (2025), local-body reservation (2019) and the patriarchy PYQ (2021) ask for interventions that shift social attitudes, not only literacy and benefit transfers.
*   ***Child protection and nutrition*** - The National Child Policy (2016) and Mid-Day Meal (2013) PYQs connect child rights to nutrition, enrolment and retention outcomes.
*   ***Delivery, identity and leakage*** - The Aadhaar PYQ (2014) frames the trade-off between last-mile targeting and exclusion errors, privacy and the voluntary-vs-compulsory debate.
*   ***Spatial and structural exclusion*** - PURA (2013) and the urban-business-exclusion PYQ (2014) raise whether protective schemes can inadvertently lock beneficiaries into segmented markets.

## Detailed Framework Breakdown

### 1. Rights-Based Approach Dimension
*   ***Entitlement vs benevolence*** - A rights-based scheme creates justiciable claims (***RTE, NFSA, MGNREGA***) rather than discretionary charity (2023).
*   ***Positive discrimination*** - Targeting the vulnerable is constitutionally mandated affirmative action (***Articles 15(3), 15(4), 16(4), 46***), not unfair discrimination (2023).
*   ***Substantive equality*** - Treating unequals equally entrenches disadvantage; differentiated support advances real equality of opportunity.
*   ***Accountability*** - Rights-based design adds grievance redress, ***social audit*** and time-bound delivery.

### 2. Targeting and Identification Dimension
*   ***Identification base*** - The ***SECC 2011*** and deprivation criteria identify beneficiaries for housing, food and pension schemes.
*   ***Inclusion vs exclusion errors*** - Tight targeting reduces cost but risks excluding the genuinely poor; universal access reduces stigma but raises cost.
*   ***Intersectionality*** - Vulnerability compounds across caste, gender, disability and location, demanding layered targeting.
*   ***Dynamic poverty*** - Households move in and out of poverty, so static lists misallocate benefits.

### 3. Awareness and Participation Dimension
*   ***Demand-side awareness*** - Low awareness of entitlements suppresses uptake - the core of the 2019 PYQ.
*   ***Participatory planning*** - Involving beneficiaries via the ***Gram Sabha*** and ***social audit*** improves design and accountability.
*   ***Information asymmetry*** - Complex procedures and documentation deter the least literate and most vulnerable.
*   ***Community mobilisation*** - ***SHGs***, NGOs and frontline workers (ASHA, anganwadi) bridge the awareness gap.

### 4. Disability Inclusion Dimension
*   ***Expanded scope*** - The ***Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016*** recognises ***21 disabilities*** (up from 7) and raises government-job reservation to ***4%*** (2017, 2022).
*   ***Accessibility*** - The Accessible India Campaign (***Sugamya Bharat***) mandates barrier-free built, transport and digital environments.
*   ***Sensitisation gap*** - Without training of functionaries and public attitude change, the statute stays a paper right (2022).
*   ***Grievance and inclusion*** - Chief Commissioner for PwD and inclusive education and employment make rights real (2017).

### 5. Gender Empowerment Dimension
*   ***Social capital*** - Women's networks, ***SHGs*** and collective agency convert isolation into bargaining power and empowerment (2025).
*   ***Political reservation*** - ***73rd and 74th Amendments*** reserve at least one-third (often ***50%***) of local-body seats for women (2019).
*   ***Proxy participation*** - 'Sarpanch-pati' capture limits the patriarchy-shifting impact of reservation (2019).
*   ***Attitudinal change*** - Beyond schemes, men's engagement, safety, media and law shift the patriarchal milieu (2021).
*   ***Economic agency*** - Asset ownership, credit and paid work underpin durable empowerment.

### 6. Child Protection Dimension
*   ***Policy frame*** - The ***National Policy for Children, 2013*** treats every person below 18 as a child with rights to survival, development, protection and participation (2016).
*   ***Mid-Day Meal*** - The ***PM POSHAN*** (Mid-Day Meal) scheme pursues twin aims - nutrition and school enrolment/retention (2013).
*   ***Institutions*** - The ***NCPCR*** and ***POCSO Act, 2012*** anchor monitoring and protection.
*   ***Convergence*** - ICDS, RTE and health programmes must converge to break early-life deprivation.

### 7. Legal Empowerment Dimension
*   ***Constitutional basis*** - ***Article 39A*** makes free legal aid a directive of equal justice; the ***Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987*** operationalises it (2023).
*   ***Who is entitled*** - Women, children, SC/STs, disabled persons, victims of trafficking, industrial workmen and those below an income ceiling.
*   ***NALSA's role*** - ***NALSA*** runs Lok Adalats, legal-literacy camps and para-legal volunteers down to the taluka level (2023).
*   ***Access to justice*** - Legal aid converts formal rights into usable remedies for the poor.

### 8. Delivery and Leakage Dimension
*   ***JAM architecture*** - ***Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile*** enables ***Direct Benefit Transfer*** and plugs leakages (2014).
*   ***Voluntary vs mandatory*** - The Aadhaar debate weighs targeting gains against exclusion errors and privacy (***Puttaswamy, 2017***).
*   ***Exclusion risk*** - Authentication failure and digital divides can deny benefits to the most vulnerable.
*   ***Last-mile delivery*** - ***DBT***, e-governance and banking correspondents shorten the delivery chain.

### 9. Social Capital Dimension
*   ***Bonding and bridging*** - Bonding ties within a group and bridging ties across groups expand resources and voice (2025).
*   ***SHG movement*** - ***DAY-NRLM*** self-help groups build savings, credit and collective bargaining for women.
*   ***Collective efficacy*** - Trust and networks lower transaction costs and improve scheme uptake and accountability.
*   ***Empowerment pathway*** - Social capital translates resources and agency into measurable achievements (***Kabeer***).

### 10. Institutional Mechanisms Dimension
*   ***Statutory and constitutional bodies*** - ***NCW, NCPCR, NCSC, NCST, NCBC*** and the Chief Commissioner for PwD monitor safeguards.
*   ***Recommendatory limits*** - Most commissions can investigate and recommend but not enforce, blunting impact.
*   ***Convergence platforms*** - Aspirational Districts and mission-mode programmes coordinate across departments.
*   ***Accountability tools*** - ***Social audit***, ***RTI*** and grievance portals strengthen enforcement.

### 11. Spatial Inclusion Dimension
*   ***PURA*** - Providing Urban Amenities in Rural Areas rests on physical, electronic, knowledge and economic connectivity (2013).
*   ***Rural-urban gap*** - Amenities and markets concentrate in cities, deepening rural disadvantage.
*   ***Market segmentation*** - Protective resourcing can lock beneficiaries into low-return rural niches, limiting urban enterprise (2014).
*   ***Migration and portability*** - Portable entitlements (***One Nation One Ration Card***) reduce exclusion of migrants.

### 12. Outcome and Convergence Dimension
*   ***Outcome orientation*** - Schemes must be judged by measurable outcomes - nutrition, learning, jobs - not allocation alone.
*   ***Macro complement*** - Welfare needs sound macro management of inflation and unemployment to actually serve the poor (2022).
*   ***Convergence*** - Overlapping schemes must align to avoid duplication and gaps.
*   ***Sustainability*** - Empowerment must outlast the scheme through assets, skills and institutions.

## Keywords, Concepts, Committees, Laws & Thinkers

### Keywords & Concepts
*   ***Rights-based approach*** - Welfare delivered as a justiciable entitlement, not charity.
*   ***Affirmative action*** - Constitutionally permitted preferential treatment of the disadvantaged.
*   ***Social capital*** - Networks and trust that expand a group's resources and agency.
*   ***JAM trinity*** - Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile, the rails of leak-proof delivery.
*   ***Direct Benefit Transfer*** - Routing benefits to bank accounts to cut leakage.
*   ***Benchmark disability*** - A 40%-or-more disability qualifying for RPwD benefits.
*   ***Social audit*** - Community verification of scheme implementation.
*   ***Legal aid*** - Free legal services for the poor under Article 39A.
*   ***Exclusion error*** - Wrongful denial of benefits to an eligible person.
*   ***Empowerment*** - Expansion of agency, resources and achievements.

### Committees & Reports
*   ***Sachar Committee (2006)*** - Assessed the socio-economic and educational status of Muslims.
*   ***Justice Verma Committee (2013)*** - Reforms on laws relating to crimes against women.
*   ***Mungekar / Standing Committees*** - Reviews of welfare and reservation policy.
*   ***2nd ARC (Social Capital report)*** - On citizen-centric, accountable service delivery.
*   ***Various Working Groups*** - On disability, child rights and women's empowerment.

### Laws, Acts & Schemes
*   ***Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016*** - Expanded disability rights, reservation and accessibility.
*   ***Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987*** - Basis of NALSA and free legal aid (Article 39A).
*   ***Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012*** - Child-protection framework.
*   ***73rd & 74th Amendments, 1992*** - Reservation of seats for women in local bodies.
*   ***National Policy for Children, 2013*** - Charter of children's rights.
*   ***Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024*** - Curbs cheating in central exams; excludes state-board/university exams.
*   ***PM POSHAN / ICDS / DAY-NRLM*** - Nutrition, early-childhood and SHG-livelihood missions.

### Thinkers & Frameworks
*   ***Amartya Sen - Capability Approach*** - Development is the expansion of people's real freedoms and capabilities, not mere income. India: The case for judging welfare schemes by capabilities and outcomes, not outlays.

## Issues Emerging from the PYQs
*   ***Marketisation vs the public system*** - The 2024 and 2015 PYQs ask how the State can contain out-of-pocket expenditure and high private-sector dependence while expanding public-health reach.
*   ***Primary and community health first*** - The 'Health for All' (2018) and primary-health (2021) PYQs root sustainable development in strong primary and community-level care, not tertiary hospitals.
*   ***Quality and equity in education*** - The RTE (2022), NEP (2020) and higher-education (2015, 2014) PYQs shift the debate from access to learning outcomes, autonomy and global competitiveness.
*   ***Education-skill-employment chain*** - The skilling (2023) and demographic-dividend (2016) PYQs test whether schooling actually converts into employable, productive skills.

## Detailed Framework Breakdown

### 1. Public Health System Dimension
*   ***State's vital role*** - Health is a public good; the State must counter the marketisation that raises cost and excludes the poor (2024).
*   ***Out-of-pocket burden*** - High out-of-pocket expenditure pushes millions into poverty each year, the core equity failure.
*   ***Grassroots reach*** - Health and Wellness Centres / Ayushman Arogya Mandirs and Ayushman Bharat extend care to the village level (2024).
*   ***Three-tier system*** - Sub-centres, PHCs and CHCs form the public delivery backbone needing staff and supplies.

### 2. Universal Health Coverage Dimension
*   ***UHC goal*** - Everyone receives needed care without financial hardship - the 2015 PYQ's benchmark.
*   ***Public-private mix*** - Insurance (PMJAY) and empanelled private hospitals can bridge gaps but need regulation.
*   ***Strategic purchasing*** - The State as a smart purchaser can expand coverage while containing cost.
*   ***Alternatives*** - Strengthening public provision, generic medicines (Jan Aushadhi) and telemedicine widen access.

### 3. Primary and Community Health Dimension
*   ***Primary-care first*** - The Alma-Ata 'Health for All' vision makes primary care the foundation (2018).
*   ***Community workers*** - ASHA, ANM and anganwadi workers deliver care and awareness at the doorstep.
*   ***Preventive and promotive*** - Sanitation, nutrition and immunisation prevent disease more cheaply than cure (2021).
*   ***Social determinants*** - Water, housing and education shape health outcomes beyond clinics.

### 4. Life-Cycle Health Dimension
*   ***Maternal health*** - JSY, JSSK and Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan target safe pregnancy and delivery (2020).
*   ***Child health*** - Immunisation (Mission Indradhanush) and nutrition reduce infant and child mortality.
*   ***Geriatric care*** - An ageing population needs the National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly and long-term care (2020).
*   ***Continuum of care*** - Health needs run across the life cycle, demanding age-specific services.

### 5. Right to Education Dimension
*   ***Constitutional right*** - Article 21A and the RTE Act, 2009 guarantee free and compulsory education for ages 6-14 (2022).
*   ***Beyond access*** - Enrolment is near-universal, but learning outcomes (ASER) remain weak, the real gap.
*   ***Incentives and awareness*** - Mid-day meals, scholarships and parental awareness drive retention (2022).
*   ***EWS provision*** - A 25% reservation for disadvantaged children in private schools widens access.

### 6. Education Quality and NEP Dimension
*   ***NEP 2020 vision*** - Restructures schooling to a 5+3+3+4 design and aligns with SDG-4 on inclusive, quality education (2020).
*   ***Foundational learning*** - NIPUN Bharat targets foundational literacy and numeracy, the system's weakest link.
*   ***Multidisciplinarity*** - Flexible curricula, mother-tongue instruction and vocational integration.
*   ***Spending target*** - NEP reiterates the long-unmet goal of 6% of GDP on education.

### 7. Higher Education and Autonomy Dimension
*   ***Institutional autonomy*** - Premier institutes need freedom over curricula, selection and finance to stay competitive (2014).
*   ***Global competitiveness*** - Few Indian universities rank globally; quality and research are the constraint (2015).
*   ***Foreign institutions*** - Their entry can raise standards and choice but risks commercialisation and access concerns (2015).
*   ***Research and innovation*** - India under-invests in R&D (~0.6-0.7% of GDP), capping higher-education quality.

### 8. Skilling and Employability Dimension
*   ***Education-skill-employment link*** - Schooling must convert into industry-relevant skills and jobs (2023).
*   ***Skill missions*** - Skill India, PMKVY and apprenticeships expand vocational capacity.
*   ***Skill mismatch*** - A gap between training and employer demand limits employability.
*   ***Quality of skilling*** - Certification without competence leaves the dividend unrealised.

### 9. Human Resource Development Dimension
*   ***Demographic dividend*** - A young population is an asset only if educated, healthy, skilled and creative (2016, 2023).
*   ***Window of opportunity*** - The dividend is time-bound; under-investment turns it into a demographic burden.
*   ***Health-education synergy*** - HRD needs both human capital pillars - health and education - together.
*   ***Creativity and aptitude*** - 21st-century jobs need problem-solving and digital skills, not rote learning.

### 10. Decentralisation Dimension
*   ***Subsidiarity*** - Decisions should sit closest to information and execution for responsive development (2025).
*   ***Local knowledge*** - Frontline and local bodies hold context that central planning lacks.
*   ***Accountability*** - Decentralised delivery improves feedback, ownership and last-mile fit.
*   ***Capacity caveat*** - Devolution needs funds, functionaries and training to work.

### 11. Human Development Outcomes Dimension
*   ***Growth-HD gap*** - High GDP growth has not lifted India's human-development indicators proportionately (2019).
*   ***Inclusive development*** - Regional, gender and caste gaps make balanced development elusive.
*   ***Sen's prescription*** - Investing in primary education and primary health is the surest route to development (2016).
*   ***Measurement*** - HDI and the MPI capture deprivation that GDP averages conceal.

### 12. Social-Sector Financing Dimension
*   ***Under-funding*** - Public health spending around ~2% of GDP lags the 2.5% National Health Policy target.
*   ***Education spending*** - Persistently below the long-stated 6% of GDP goal.
*   ***Quality of spend*** - Outcomes depend on how funds are used, not allocation alone.
*   ***Centre-state share*** - States deliver most social services, so transfers and own-revenue matter.

# Framework 11: THEME 3 - ISSUES RELATED TO POVERTY AND HUNGER

### Hierarchy
[Hierarchy 1] [Subject: SOCIAL JUSTICE] [Section Group: Poverty & Hunger] [Microtopic: Issues and Challenges relating to Poverty & Hunger] [Subtopic: Poverty]
[Hierarchy 2] [Subject: SOCIAL JUSTICE] [Section Group: Poverty & Hunger] [Microtopic: Issues and Challenges relating to Poverty & Hunger] [Subtopic: Hunger]

## Issues Emerging from the PYQs
*   ***Poverty as a distributional problem*** - The 2025 PYQ locates poverty in the unequal ownership of resources - the 'paradox of poverty' amid plenty - not merely in low growth.
*   ***The poverty-malnutrition-human-capital trap*** - The 2024 and 2021 PYQs frame a vicious cycle in which malnutrition stunts human-capital formation and perpetuates poverty.
*   ***Measuring poverty beyond income*** - The MPI PYQ (2020) and the estimates PYQ (2015) test whether incidence and intensity across multiple deprivations beat a single income line.
*   ***Hunger as entitlement, not availability*** - The 2018 PYQ echoes Sen: hunger persists despite food availability because of entitlement and human-development failures.
*   ***The poverty-hunger divergence*** - The 2019 PYQ shows shrinking social expenditure squeezing food budgets as the poor spend more on non-food essentials.
*   ***Political will and programme performance*** - The 2017 PYQs ask whether poverty programmes are show-pieces without political will and good governance.
*   ***Gender at the centre*** - The SHG PYQ (2021) puts women's economic agency at the core of breaking the cycle.

## Framework Overview
1. ***Poverty Measurement Dimension***
2. ***Multidimensional Poverty Dimension***
3. ***Inequality and Assets Dimension***
4. ***Vicious-Cycle Dimension***
5. ***Hunger and Nutrition Dimension***
6. ***Entitlement Dimension***
7. ***Food Security Delivery Dimension***
8. ***Poverty Alleviation Programmes Dimension***
9. ***Gender Dimension***
10. ***Governance and Political-Will Dimension***

## Detailed Framework Breakdown

### 1. Poverty Measurement Dimension
*   ***Income/consumption line*** - The Tendulkar and Rangarajan committees set successive poverty lines, with differing head-counts (2015).
*   ***Urban-rural divide*** - Poverty lines and trends differ sharply across urban and rural India (2015).
*   ***Estimation disputes*** - Different methods all show decline but disagree on magnitude.
*   ***Beyond the line*** - A single line misses depth, vulnerability and non-income deprivation.

### 2. Multidimensional Poverty Dimension
*   ***Incidence and intensity*** - The MPI combines the head-count (incidence) with the depth of deprivation (intensity), unlike an income line (2020).
*   ***Three dimensions*** - The global MPI (UNDP/OPHI) measures health, education and standard of living across 10 indicators.
*   ***Alkire-Foster method*** - Counts overlapping deprivations a household faces simultaneously.
*   ***National MPI*** - NITI Aayog's National MPI reports a sharp fall in multidimensional poverty in recent years.

### 3. Inequality and Assets Dimension
*   ***Asset inequality*** - Unequal ownership of land, capital and credit reproduces poverty despite growth (2025).
*   ***Paradox of poverty*** - Deprivation persists amid aggregate plenty when gains concentrate at the top.
*   ***Wealth vs income gap*** - Wealth inequality is typically far sharper than income inequality.
*   ***Redistribution*** - Land reform, progressive tax and asset transfers address the structural root.

### 4. Vicious-Cycle Dimension
*   ***Nurkse's vicious circle*** - Low income → low saving and nutrition → low productivity → low income (2024).
*   ***Human-capital erosion*** - Malnutrition causes stunting and learning loss, lowering lifetime earnings (2024).
*   ***Early-life window*** - The first 1,000 days are decisive for breaking the cycle.
*   ***Breaking points*** - Nutrition, health, education and women's income jointly snap the loop.

### 5. Hunger and Nutrition Dimension
*   ***Malnutrition burden*** - High stunting, wasting and anaemia persist despite food sufficiency.
*   ***Hidden hunger*** - Micronutrient deficiency harms health even where calories suffice.
*   ***Nutrition missions*** - POSHAN Abhiyaan, ICDS and PM POSHAN target the nutrition deficit.
*   ***Diet diversity*** - Non-cereal, protein-rich diets matter as much as calories.

### 6. Entitlement Dimension
*   ***Entitlement, not availability*** - Sen showed famines arise from entitlement failure, not absolute food shortage (2018).
*   ***Human-development focus*** - Hunger reflects weak health, education and livelihood policies, not just food stocks (2018).
*   ***Purchasing power*** - Income, employment and prices determine access to available food.
*   ***Rights-based response*** - NFSA converts food access into a legal entitlement.

### 7. Food Security Delivery Dimension
*   ***NFSA coverage*** - The National Food Security Act, 2013 covers up to 75% rural and 50% urban population (2019).
*   ***Squeezed food budgets*** - Rising non-food essential costs force the poor to cut food spending (2019).
*   ***PDS reform*** - DBT, end-to-end computerisation and One Nation One Ration Card cut leakage and aid migrants.
*   ***Buffer and procurement*** - MSP, procurement and buffer stocks underpin food availability.

### 8. Poverty Alleviation Programmes Dimension
*   ***Flagship programmes*** - MGNREGA, NRLM, PMAY and NSAP form the anti-poverty architecture (2017).
*   ***Wage employment*** - MGNREGA guarantees 100 days of work, a demand-driven safety net.
*   ***Political will*** - Without sustained will and good governance, programmes become show-pieces (2017).
*   ***Leakage and targeting*** - Identification errors and diversion blunt impact.

### 9. Gender Dimension
*   ***Feminisation of poverty*** - Women bear poverty and malnutrition disproportionately (2021).
*   ***SHG microfinance*** - DAY-NRLM SHGs provide credit, savings and agency to break the cycle (2021).
*   ***Intergenerational effect*** - Empowered mothers improve child nutrition and schooling.
*   ***Limits*** - Microfinance needs livelihoods, markets and over-indebtedness safeguards.

### 10. Governance and Political-Will Dimension
*   ***Good governance*** - Tackling poverty and hunger is the central test of governance (2017).
*   ***Convergence*** - Anti-poverty success needs alignment across employment, food, health and education.
*   ***Accountability*** - Social audit, transparency and grievance redress drive performance.
*   ***State capacity*** - Frontline staffing and last-mile delivery determine outcomes.

## Keywords, Concepts, Committees, Laws & Thinkers

### Keywords & Concepts
*   ***Paradox of poverty*** - Deprivation persisting amid aggregate abundance.
*   ***Multidimensional Poverty Index*** - Poverty measured across health, education and living standards.
*   ***Incidence and intensity*** - Head-count of the poor and the depth of their deprivation.
*   ***Entitlement approach*** - Hunger from access failure, not food shortage (Sen).
*   ***Vicious cycle of poverty*** - Self-reinforcing low income, nutrition and productivity.
*   ***Stunting*** - Low height-for-age from chronic malnutrition.
*   ***Feminisation of poverty*** - The disproportionate poverty burden on women.
*   ***Asset inequality*** - Unequal ownership of land, capital and credit.
*   ***NFSA*** - Legal right to subsidised foodgrains.
*   ***Social expenditure*** - Public spending on health, education and welfare.

### Committees & Reports
*   ***Tendulkar Committee (2009)*** - Revised the poverty line using a wider consumption basket.
*   ***Rangarajan Committee (2014)*** - Suggested a higher poverty line than Tendulkar.
*   ***Suresh Tendulkar / C. Rangarajan estimates*** - Successive official poverty head-counts.
*   ***NITI Aayog National MPI*** - Measures multidimensional poverty across states.
*   ***Saxena Committee*** - On BPL identification criteria.

### Laws, Acts & Schemes
*   ***National Food Security Act, 2013*** - Legal right to subsidised foodgrains for two-thirds of the population.
*   ***MGNREGA, 2005*** - Guarantees 100 days of rural wage employment.
*   ***DAY-NRLM / DAY-NULM*** - SHG-based rural and urban livelihood missions.
*   ***PMAY / NSAP / PM-KISAN*** - Housing, social-assistance pensions and income support.
*   ***POSHAN Abhiyaan / ICDS*** - National nutrition mission and child-development services.
*   ***One Nation One Ration Card*** - Portable food-security entitlements for migrants.

### Thinkers & Frameworks
*   ***Amartya Sen - Entitlement Approach*** - Famine and hunger arise from a collapse of entitlements (access), not from an absolute lack of food. India: The decisive answer to the 2018 food-availability PYQ.
*   ***Sabina Alkire & James Foster - Multidimensional Poverty*** - Poverty should count overlapping deprivations in health, education and living standards, capturing incidence and intensity. India: The method behind the MPI PYQ (2020).
*   ***Ragnar Nurkse - Vicious Circle of Poverty*** - Low income causes low saving and productivity, which keep income low - a self-perpetuating trap. India: Frames the poverty-malnutrition cycle PYQs (2024, 2021).
*   ***John Rawls - Difference Principle*** - Inequalities are just only if they most benefit the least-advantaged. India: A normative yardstick for asset inequality and the paradox of poverty (2025).
*   ***Muhammad Yunus - Microfinance*** - Small collateral-free credit to the poor, especially women, can unlock self-employment and dignity. India: The basis for the SHG-microfinance PYQ (2021).

*   **Mobilisation Tactics**
    *   Participation in movements: Non-Cooperation Movement, Civil Disobedience Movement, Quit India Movement
    *   Grassroots leadership (local satyagrahas, protests)

*   **Examples:**
    *   Mahatma Gandhi's satyagraha gaining global admiration
    *   Brutality of British exposed in Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
    *   Women/elderly participation creating moral pressure (e.g., salt marches led by ordinary citizens)

### 4. Economic Dimension
*   **Economic Tactics**
    *   Boycott of foreign goods
    *   Promotion of swadeshi, khadi
    *   Participation in constructive programmes
    *   Labour strikes, peasant resistance (tax refusal)
    *   Undermining colonial economic base
*   **Examples:**
    *   Boycott of Manchester cloth; promotion of khadi
    *   Refusal to pay taxes in Bardoli under Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
    *   Strikes by textile workers in Bombay during nationalist phase

### 5. Social Reform & Transformation
*   **Social Shifts**
    *   Challenged patriarchy, caste, orthodoxy
    *   Rise of new social identities (women as political actors, peasants as citizens)
    *   Education & awareness spread
    *   Participation in public sphere
    *   Social emancipation parallel to political freedom
*   **Examples:**
    *   Women entering public life (e.g., Kasturba Gandhi in satyagrahas)
    *   Anti-untouchability campaigns led by Gandhi
    *   Spread of basic education via national schools during NCM

### 6. Cultural & Symbolic Contribution
*   **Cultural Expressions**
    *   Use of symbols: khadi, charkha, national songs
    *   Literature, poetry, theatre
    *   Revival of indigenous pride
    *   National festivals, processions
    *   Creation of shared national identity
*   **Examples:**
    *   Charkha & khadi as symbols of self-reliance
    *   Patriotic songs like "Vande Mataram" mobilising masses
    *   Public celebrations of Ganesh Utsav/Shivaji festivals (earlier started by Bal Gangadhar Tilak but continued impact)

### 7. Organisational & Leadership Role

    *   Limited rural penetration in early phases (pre-Gandhian)
