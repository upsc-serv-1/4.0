Q1. Which of the following heart sounds are best heard with the bell of stethoscope?

I. Opening snap
II. Systolic click
III. Third heart sound
IV. Mid diastolic murmur

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

(a) I and IV
(b) II and III only
(c) III and IV
(d) I, II and III

1. Solution (c)

Explanation: The bell of the stethoscope is designed to pick up LOW-FREQUENCY sounds. Third heart sound (S3) is a low-pitched, low-frequency sound best heard with the bell at the cardiac apex. Mid-diastolic murmur (e.g., in mitral stenosis) is also low frequency and best heard with the bell. Opening snap and systolic click are HIGH-frequency sounds best heard with the diaphragm.

Q2. Consider the following statements for diagnosing ventricular aneurysm in a patient with recent myocardial infarction:

I. Paradoxical impulse on chest wall
II. Persistent ST elevation on ECG
III. Unusual bulge from cardiac silhouette on X-ray
IV. Presence of pulsus paradoxsus

Which of the above are correct?

(a) I and II only
(b) I and IV
(c) I, II and III
(d) II, III and IV

2. Solution (c)

Explanation: Ventricular aneurysm post-MI is diagnosed by: (I) Paradoxical (dyskinetic) impulse on the chest wall – the aneurysmal segment bulges outward during systole instead of contracting inward; (II) Persistent ST elevation on ECG beyond 3–4 weeks post-MI – a classic ECG hallmark of LV aneurysm; (III) Unusual bulge from cardiac silhouette on CXR. Pulsus paradoxus (IV) is a feature of cardiac tamponade or constrictive pericarditis, NOT ventricular aneurysm.

Q4. Which one of the following is correct with regard to Carey Coombs murmur?

(a) Soft systolic murmur due to mitral regurgitation
(b) Soft mid-diastolic murmur due to mitral valvulitis
(c) Harsh early diastolic murmur due to aortic regurgitation
(d) Blowing late systolic murmur due to aortic stenosis

4. Solution (b)

Explanation: Carey Coombs murmur is a soft, rumbling MID-DIASTOLIC murmur heard at the apex in acute rheumatic carditis. It is due to thickening and edema of the mitral valve leaflets (mitral valvulitis) causing relative mitral stenosis. It is a transient murmur that disappears as the acute inflammation subsides. It must be distinguished from the diastolic murmur of established mitral stenosis.

Q5. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the Opening Snap (OS) in a patient of mitral stenosis?

(a) OS is best heard with the bell of stethoscope
(b) Intensity of OS becomes louder when the valve is calcified
(c) OS moves closer to the second sound (S2) as the stenosis becomes more severe
(d) OS is best heard at the second left intercostal space

5. Solution (c)

Explanation: Opening Snap (OS) in mitral stenosis: OS is a HIGH-frequency sound best heard with the DIAPHRAGM (not the bell). As MS becomes MORE SEVERE, left atrial pressure is higher → the mitral valve opens earlier → S2-OS interval SHORTENS (OS moves closer to S2). A short S2-OS interval (<0.08 sec) indicates severe MS. When the valve is CALCIFIED (rigid), the OS may DISAPPEAR because the stiffened leaflets cannot snap open. OS is best heard at the cardiac APEX or left sternal border, not at the second left ICS (which is the pulmonary area).

Q6. Which one of the following responses to intravenous adenosine is correctly matched?

Arrhythmia - Response

(a) Atrial fibrillation – Termination
(b) Atrio-ventricular nodal reentrant tachycardia – Termination
(c) Ventricular tachycardia – Termination and complete recovery
(d) Atrial flutter – Termination and complete recovery

6. Solution (b)

Explanation: Adenosine is an endogenous nucleoside that causes transient AV nodal block. AVNRT (AV Nodal Reentrant Tachycardia) – the reentrant circuit depends on the AV node; adenosine TERMINATES it (drug of choice). Atrial fibrillation – adenosine does NOT terminate AF; it may transiently slow ventricular rate. Atrial flutter – adenosine does NOT terminate AFL but can unmask flutter waves by slowing conduction. Ventricular tachycardia – adenosine is generally INEFFECTIVE against VT (except adenosine-sensitive idiopathic VT from RVOT).

II. Remove the cap and shake the inhaler
III. Breathe out gently and place the mouthpiece into the mouth
IV. Hold the breath for 10 seconds

Which one of the following is the correct sequence of using MDI?

(b) II, I, IV, III, V
(c) II, III, I, V, IV

Q27. A 52 year old male has uncontrolled diabetes. Which one of the following tests will help in early detection of nephropathy?

(a) Blood urea level
(b) Serum creatinine level
(c) Ultrasonography
(d) Urine albumin

27. Solution (d)

II. The vaccine should be used within 2 hours of reconstitution or opening
III. Past history of intussusception is a contraindication
IV. The vaccine can be given if the baby has ongoing diarrhoea

Select the answer using the code given below:

(a) I and II
(b) III and IV
(c) I and III
(d) II and IV

111. Solution (c)

Explanation: Oral Rotavirus Vaccine (Rotarix/RotaTeq): (I) TRUE – Cold chain: Storage at 2–8°C (refrigerator temperature). (II) FALSE – After opening/reconstitution, the vaccine should be used within 4 hours (not 2 hours). (III) TRUE – Past history of intussusception is an ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION (rotavirus vaccine slightly increases risk of intussusception; contraindicated if prior history). (IV) FALSE – Ongoing diarrhoea is a PRECAUTION (defer vaccination until illness resolves; concerns about reduced immune response). Hence I and III are correct.

I. The baby should be initiated on breastfeeding within one hour of birth

Q115. Which one of the following oral drugs may be used in the management of super-refractory status epilepticus?

(a) Clonazepam
(b) Clobazam
(c) Lamotrigine
(d) Topiramate

115. Solution (d)

Explanation: Super-refractory status epilepticus (SRSE): SE continuing or recurring ≥24 hours after the onset of anesthetic therapy. Treatment options for SRSE: IV anesthetics (thiopental, propofol, midazolam infusion). ORAL/ENTERAL agents tried in SRSE: TOPIRAMATE (d): Oral, broad-spectrum AED; used as add-on in SRSE via nasogastric tube. Pyridoxine, ketamine, immunotherapy (steroids, IVIG). Clobazam (b): Oral benzodiazepine used for chronic epilepsy, not specifically for SRSE. Lamotrigine and Clonazepam: Not preferred in acute SRSE management. Topiramate is the established oral option for super-refractory SE.

Q117. Which of the following statements are correct regarding hypernatremia in children?

I. Diabetes insipidus due to a deficiency of antidiuretic hormone (ADH) may cause hypernatremia
II. Addison disease may be associated with hypernatremia
III. Use of boiled skimmed milk can lead to hypernatremia
IV. Use of lactulose can lead to hypernatremia

Select the answer using the code given below:

(a) I, III and IV
(b) II, III and IV
(c) I, II and III
(d) I, II and IV

117. Solution (a)

Explanation: Hypernatremia in children: (I) TRUE – Central DI (ADH deficiency): Loss of free water → hypernatremia. (II) FALSE – Addison's disease (adrenal insufficiency): HYPONATREMIA (aldosterone deficiency → Na+ wasting) and hyperkalemia. NOT hypernatremia. Q118. Which of the following statements are correct regarding the management of gastrointestinal bleeding in children?

I. Somatostatin or octreotide infusion should be given for at least 7 days after stoppage of initial bleeding to prevent rebleeding
II. Endoscopic Sclerotherapy (EST) involves endoscopic injection of N-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate or iso-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate
III. EST has upto 90% efficacy in controlling acute bleeding
IV. Following an episode of acute variceal bleeding, all patients should receive secondary prophylaxis to prevent rebleeding

Select the answer using the code given below:

(a) I and II
(b) I and III
(c) III and IV
(d) II and IV

118. Solution (c)

Explanation: GI bleeding management in children: (I) FALSE – Somatostatin/octreotide infusion: Standard duration is 3–5 DAYS (not 7 days) after acute variceal bleed. Q119. Which of the following are advantages of endotracheal intubation, in a child requiring pediatric advanced life support?

I. Inspiratory time can be controlled
II. Positive end-expiratory pressure can be provided
III. Peak expiratory pressure can be controlled
IV. Reduced risk of aspiration of gastric contents

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

(a) I, II and III
(b) I, II and IV
(c) I, III and IV
(d) II, III and IV

119. Solution (b)

Explanation: Advantages of Endotracheal Intubation (ETT) in PALS: (I) TRUE – Inspiratory time, rate, and tidal volume can all be precisely CONTROLLED with a ventilator. "Peak expiratory pressure" is not a standard ventilator-controlled parameter. (IV) TRUE – ETT with cuff PROTECTS the airway → REDUCES aspiration risk. Hence I, II, and IV are correct.

Q120. Which of the following are causes of hypocalcemia in a child?

I. Hypomagnesemia
II. Hypophosphatemia
III. Metabolic acidosis
IV. Pseudohypoparathyroidism

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

(a) I and II
(b) II and III
(c) III and IV
(d) I and IV

120. Solution (d)

Explanation: Causes of hypocalcemia: (I) Hypomagnesemia – TRUE: Mg²⁺ is needed for PTH secretion and action; severe hypomagnesemia → functional hypoparathyroidism → hypocalcemia. (II) Hypophosphatemia – FALSE: Hypophosphatemia is associated with NORMAL or HIGH calcium; high Ca×P product causes hypocalcemia (hyperphosphatemia → hypocalcemia). (III) Metabolic acidosis – FALSE: Acidosis INCREASES ionized calcium (H+ displaces Ca²⁺ from albumin → MORE free ionized Ca²⁺). Alkalosis causes hypocalcemia (tetany in hyperventilation). (IV) Pseudohypoparathyroidism – TRUE: End-organ resistance to PTH → PTH is elevated but ineffective → hypocalcemia + hyperphosphatemia. Hence I and IV are correct.
